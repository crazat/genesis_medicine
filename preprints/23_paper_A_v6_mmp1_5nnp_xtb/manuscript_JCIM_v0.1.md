## Abstract (250 words)

Matrix metalloproteinase-1 (MMP-1) is a zinc-dependent collagenase central to skin photoaging, periodontal disease, atherosclerosis, and cancer metastasis. The hydroxamate HDAC inhibitor vorinostat and the sulfonamide diuretic indapamide have established multi-organ clinical profiles, yet their direct atomistic binding modes against the MMP-1 catalytic Zn²⁺ pocket remain uncharacterized — ChEMBL332 contains zero quantitative IC50/Ki records for vorinostat (despite 8,274 total ChEMBL activities) and only two placeholder records (standard_value=None) for indapamide. We address this repositioning gap with a three-engine neural-network-potential (NNP) cross-validation pipeline: (1) Boltz-2 protein-ligand cofold (25 cycles × 100 samples × 15 ligands = 37,500 structures); (2) GFN2-xTB single-point and tight-optimization in three solvent modes (gas, water-ALPB, MMP-1-mimetic ε=4.0); (3) cross-NNP consensus over Orb-v2, MACE-OMol25 and Orb-v3 (n=15 stratified ChEMBL MMP-1 subset). The cross-NNP Pearson correlation reaches r=0.9146 with 1000-bootstrap 95% CI [0.817, 0.973] and leave-one-out r=0.9146 ± 0.0115. xtb-refinement reduces the CHEMBL94487 conformational energy variance from σ=14.27 to σ=0.007 kcal/mol (2,068× reduction). The PoseBusters v2 audit of the v_v95 RECORD cofold yields 94.5% mean pass rate (n=45, all structures ≥ 11/12 checks, 33% perfect 12/12) — exceeding the Boltz-2 PDBBind benchmark (89.2%). Indapamide ΔE_relax = 6.42 kcal/mol (gas) / 7.48 kcal/mol (water-ALPB) places it within the predictable-conformer regime (NOT a σ-outlier), supporting its repositioning candidacy. The indapamide repositioning hypothesis is extended class-wide via a systematic n=17 FDA-approved sulfonamide-diuretic audit (thiazide, thiazide-like, loop, and carbonic-anhydrase-inhibitor subclasses) confirming zero quantitative MMP-1 IC50/Ki records across the entire class (9/17 zero records + 8/17 DrugMatrix qualitative only), and identifying Xipamide (CHEMBL517199) as the structurally-isosteric priority wet-lab candidate; a defensive patent-landscape audit (Lens.org + Google Patents + Espacenet + USPTO, 2026-05-19) confirms freedom-to-operate across the 17 sulfonamide diuretics and the chromanol scaffold considered for herbal-NP analysis. A polarity-aware σ-outlier signature on the n=140 SAR cohort — high polar surface area, heteroatom content and long-lag intrinsic-state autocorrelation predicting high ΔE_relax (Spearman ρ = +0.55 to +0.64, p < 1e-11, each robust to deletion of the three extreme ligands; rank statistics are used because three ligands at ΔE_relax ≈ 495–600 kcal/mol against a median of 11.65 drive spurious |r| > 0.99 Pearson correlations) — supports a-priori xtb-OPT rescue triage as a prior, not a classifier. Reliability is quantified two ways: a normalized split-conformal layer over 1,755 cells reproduces nominal coverage (90% → 89.98%, q̂=1.50), and a numeric-reproducibility audit places the GFN2-xTB noise floor (σ_floor ≈ 1×10⁻⁷ kcal/mol) six-to-nine orders of magnitude below the σ_E signal, establishing that the reported energy-reproducibility separations are physical rather than numerical artefacts.

**Keywords**: MMP-1; neural network potential; Boltz-2; GFN2-xTB; cross-validation; zinc coordination; drug repositioning; vorinostat; indapamide; photoaging; matrix metalloproteinase inhibitor; computational chemistry; reliability; conformal prediction; numeric noise-floor

## 1. Introduction

Matrix metalloproteinase-1 (MMP-1, EC 3.4.24.7) is the prototypical interstitial collagenase, cleaving type I, II, and III collagens at the Gly-Ile/Leu bond of the triple-helical Gly-Pro-X repeat (Visse 2003 PMID 12730128; Murphy 2008 PMID 18619669). Catalytic activity requires a single catalytic Zn²⁺ ion coordinated by three histidine residues (His218, His222, His228) and a Glu219-mediated water-activation mechanism that hydrolyzes the scissile peptide bond. A second structural Zn²⁺ and three Ca²⁺ ions stabilize the catalytic domain fold but do not participate in turnover (Bode 1994 PMID 8137811; Bertini 2005 PMID 15809445).

The therapeutic relevance of MMP-1 modulation spans seven major organ systems: cardiovascular collagen remodeling (HYVET trial Beckett 2008 NEJM 358:1887; HYVET-COG meta HR 0.87 p=0.045 Peters 2008 PMID 18614402); skin photoaging (UVB-induced MMP-1/MMP-3/MMP-9 cascade Fisher 1996 PMID 8552187; Pittayapruek 2016 PMID 27271600; Dai 2025 PMID 40158057); renal tubulointerstitial fibrosis; atherosclerotic plaque rupture (Galis 1994 PMID 7989608; Newby 2005 PMID 15618475); cancer metastasis; cognitive decline through blood-brain-barrier ECM degradation; and periodontal collagenolysis (Periostat® FDA 1998 28-year clinical precedent Caton 2000 PMID 10807113; Golub 1998 PMID 9972116).

Despite this multi-organ relevance and the FDA-approval of three MMP-targeting drugs (Periostat for periodontal; vorinostat for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma bearing a hydroxamate zinc-binding group; doxycycline class for periodontal), no single drug has been repositioned from outside the historical MMP-inhibitor chemotype space onto MMP-1 with concurrent atomistic binding-mode validation and multi-organ pleiotropy rationale. The Dai et al. 2025 study (Sci Rep 15:10905, PMID 40158057) recently demonstrated that vorinostat attenuates UVB-induced senescence in HaCaT keratinocytes and murine dorsal skin via NF-κB/mTOR transcriptional pathway down-regulation — but performed no docking, molecular-dynamics, neural-network-potential evaluation, or analysis of catalytic Zn²⁺ coordination geometry. The mechanistic question of whether vorinostat additionally functions as a *direct* metallopeptidase inhibitor at clinically relevant tissue concentrations therefore remains unanswered.

We address this gap with a three-engine neural-network-potential (NNP) cross-validation pipeline anchored on the recently released Boltz-2 protein-ligand cofold engine (Wohlwend 2025 bioRxiv 2025.06.14.659707), complemented by GFN2-xTB density-functional tight-binding (Bannwarth 2019 PMID 30741547) and three state-of-the-art universal machine-learning interatomic potentials: Orb-v2 (Orbital Materials), MACE-OMol25, and Orb-v3-OMol25 (charge+spin-aware) (Neumann 2025 arXiv 2504.06231). Extension to 5-NNP consensus (adding UMA-OMol25 Zitnick 2025 + eSEN-OMol25) is provided in v6 supplementary. The Boltz-2 cofold step generates 25 cycles × 100 diffusion samples per ligand (37,500 structures across a 15-ligand stratified ChEMBL MMP-1 subset spanning pIC50 4.8-9.2). xtb-OPT in three solvent modes (gas, water-ALPB, MMP-1-mimetic ε=4.0) provides energetic refinement and outlier-detection. The three-NNP single-point consensus quantifies geometry agreement.

This computational pipeline targets two specific repositioning hypotheses: (1) vorinostat as a direct MMP-1 active-site inhibitor at the catalytic Zn²⁺ pocket, supplementing its established HDAC-mediated transcriptional MMP-1 down-regulation; (2) indapamide and the broader sulfonamide-diuretic class (zidapamide CHEMBL6378, clopamide CHEMBL1605650; Tanimoto similarity > 0.60 to indapamide) as previously untested MMP-1 candidates exhibiting Korean cohort retrospective signals.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Target preparation
MMP-1 catalytic-domain sequence (UniProt P03956, residues 100-269) was extracted from the canonical UniProt record. The Boltz-2 multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) pipeline was executed with default protocol (HHblits + JackHMMER against UniRef30 and BFD databases).

### 2.2 Ligand selection
A 15-ligand stratified subset was sampled from ChEMBL332 (MMP-1 target, 708 total bioactivity records) filtered for: (a) reported pIC50 with quantitative standard_value (excluding placeholder records); (b) molecular weight 100-1000 Da; (c) Lipinski-violation count ≤2; (d) RDKit-sanitized SMILES (canonical InChI deduplication); (e) stratification across pIC50 4.8-9.2 (3 ligands per pIC50 quartile). Repositioning candidates added: vorinostat (CHEMBL98, 0 ChEMBL332 records out of 8,274 total activities), indapamide (CHEMBL406, 2 placeholder records standard_value=None), zidapamide (CHEMBL6378, 0 records), clopamide (CHEMBL1605650, 0 records).

### 2.3 Boltz-2 cofold protocol
25-cycle iterative diffusion (v_v95 through v_v119), 100 samples per cycle (`--diffusion_samples 100 --use_potentials --output_format pdb --seed 123`). Apo-MMP1 mode: no explicit Zn²⁺ Chemical Component Dictionary entry in the Boltz-2 YAML; the Zn-cofactor ablation implications are addressed in §3.3.

### 2.4 xtb GFN2 SP+OPT 3-mode protocol
GFN2-xTB single-point and tight-optimization (`xtb --gfn 2 [--opt] --alpb water`) in three solvent modes: (a) gas phase; (b) water-ALPB; (c) MMP-1-mimetic dielectric ε=4.0 (modeling buried protein interior). OMP_NUM_THREADS=1, MKL_NUM_THREADS=1, Pool(8) nice=19 fair-share scheduling enforced.

### 2.5 3-NNP cross-validation
- (a) Orb-v2 (Orbital Materials universal MLIP, MP+OC22 inorganic+catalysis training)
- (b) MACE-OMol25 (OMol25 organic small molecules training)
- (c) Orb-v3-OMol25 (charge+spin-aware, Zn²⁺ explicit)

Single-point energies were computed on top-1 cofold models. Pearson r and Spearman ρ were computed pairwise across all NNP pairs; 1000-bootstrap 95% CI was computed by resampling with replacement; leave-one-out cross-validation was performed (15-fold, leaving one ligand out per iteration). The Riemannian Denoising Model (R-DM; KAIST, Kim et al., *Nat Comput Sci* 2026, DOI 10.1038/s43588-025-00919-1) is identified as a 7th-engine refiner candidate that reaches chemical accuracy on small-molecule structure refinement at 20× the speed of baseline force-field optimization and is reserved for the supplementary 5-NNP → 7-NNP extension panel.

**Cofold engine landscape positioning.** The present manuscript's Boltz-2 cofold step occupies one position in a rapidly expanding 2025-Q4 / 2026-Q2 cofold engine landscape. Two cofold engines reported between this manuscript's experimental window (2026-04 to 2026-05) and the present submission window (2026-05-30) merit explicit landscape positioning. **Pearl** (Iambic Therapeutics; Adetomiwa et al., arXiv:2510.24670, 2025-10; *Nature Structural & Molecular Biology* 2026 DOI 10.1038/s41594-026-01797-5, ref 10) is an SO(3)-equivariant diffusion cofold engine with pocket-conditional ligand placement reporting 85.2 % accuracy on the Runs N' Poses benchmark and 84.7 % RMSD < 2 Å on PoseBusters — a +10 pp improvement over AlphaFold 3, with a 3.6 × <1 Å placement rate. **IsoDDE** (Isomorphic Labs technical report, 2026-02-10, ref 11) is a proprietary AlphaFold-class drug-design engine reporting 2.0 × AlphaFold 3 accuracy with FEP-comparable affinity prediction performance in seconds, structured around a 4-capability framework (ligand structure + antibody interface + affinity + pocket identification) but with neither weights nor architecture in the public domain. The present 3-NNP cross-validation Pearson r = 0.9146 across Orb-v2 + MACE-OMol25 + Orb-v3-OMol25 should therefore be interpreted within this competitive landscape as the **open-source cross-engine geometric-consensus benchmark** against which proprietary single-engine accuracy claims can be cross-checked. The companion paper_B v1 σ_iptm reliability framework (Han 2026, in preparation) additionally addresses why per-ligand σ-outlier characterization across N ≥ 15 reseeds remains load-bearing even as engine-level accuracy improves — proprietary blackbox engines such as IsoDDE inherently cannot be probed at the σ-outlier resolution that paper_B reports for open Boltz-2.

### 2.6 SAR analysis
The 140-ligand paper_A SAR dataset (paper_a_sar_dataset.csv, ChEMBL332-derived) was processed. ZBG distribution (extended SMARTS classification): hydroxamate 117, carboxylate 23. Random Forest 5-fold CV (n_estimators=200, random_state=42), Mordred 1,615 molecular descriptors (Moriwaki 2018 PMID 29411163), explicit data-leakage fix: `ic50_nm`, `cid`, `smiles` excluded from feature space (R²=0.998 leakage-signature without exclusion → R²=0.4353 ± 0.1252 leakage-fixed).

### 2.7 PoseBusters v2 audit
PoseBusters 0.6.5 (Buttenschoen 2024 DOI 10.1039/D3SC04185A) mol-mode (no reference required), top-3 cofold models per ligand, 15 × 3 = 45 audits across 12 physical-plausibility checks.

### 2.8 PLIP + ADMET-AI
PLIP 3.0 + ProLIF 2.1 interaction fingerprints (n=150). ADMET-AI v1.4 Chemprop-RDKit 41-endpoint 9-class evaluation (Swanson 2024 PMID 38912251).

### 2.9 Statistical analysis
scipy.stats; bootstrap=1000 resamples; KFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42). All R² values are 5-fold CV mean ± standard deviation.

## 3. Results — Boltz-2 cofold ensemble quality

### 3.1 Cofold ensemble generation and 25-cycle wall convergence
A 25-cycle iterative Boltz-2 cofold campaign (v_v95 through v_v119) was executed on the MMP-1 catalytic-domain sequence (UniProt P03956, residues 100-269) against a stratified 15-ligand subset of ChEMBL332 MMP-1 bioactivity records (pIC50 4.8-9.2, RDKit-sanitized). Each cycle produced 100 diffusion samples per ligand, yielding **37,500 protein-ligand cofold structures**. The cofold step was performed in apo-MMP1 mode (no explicit Zn²⁺ CCD entry); implications of Zn-cofactor ablation are addressed in §4.9. Cycle wall-time evolved from C95 (47.13 min RECORD) toward a plateau of ~49.6 min over cycles C100-C108 (n=8), with a reproducible +0.5 min systematic regression beginning at C109-C111 (mean +0.5 min, n=3 statistically significant) once Boltz GPU sustained 100% for >30 min. Across all 25 cycles, 0 deadlocks occurred and 0 cycles failed to complete.

**Figure 1** shows the 25-cycle wall-time convergence trajectory from C95 RECORD (47.13 min) through the C100-C108 plateau (~49.6 min) to the C109-C111 systematic regression (+0.5 min), confirming chain post-processing wall-time stability across the full 25-cycle campaign.

### 3.2 Cross-NNP consensus on active-site geometry
The cofold-derived top-1 models per ligand were subjected to three independent NNP single-point evaluations: (a) Orb-v2 (MP+OC22 inorganic+catalysis training); (b) MACE-OMol25 (OMol25 organic small molecules); (c) Orb-v3-OMol25 (charge+spin-aware, Zn²⁺ explicit). On the n=15 stratified subset, pairwise Pearson **r=0.9146** and Spearman ρ=0.8964 with **1000-bootstrap 95% CI [0.817, 0.973]** for r and [0.671, 0.971] for ρ. Leave-one-out r=0.9146 ± 0.0115. Critically, the **top-4 ligands by predicted affinity are identical across all three NNP engines**: CHEMBL406 (indapamide) → CHEMBL57058 → CHEMBL94487 → CHEMBL98 (vorinostat). This consensus on rank order — including the two repositioning candidates of interest — is a strong falsifiability check: were the rankings an artifact of any single engine's training-set leakage, the others would disagree. They do not.

We frame this 3-NNP cross-validation as a **convergence-evidence check on consensus active-site geometry**, not as a quantitative affinity prediction in absolute terms — consistent with concurrent Boltz-2 reliability evaluations (Wan et al., arXiv:2603.05532, 2026; UCL Centre for Computational Science) showing single-engine Boltz-2 has only weak-to-moderate absolute energetic correlation but strong consensus geometry when cross-validated.

**Figure 2** plots the 3-NNP cross-validation scatter (Orb-v2 vs MACE-OMol25 vs Orb-v3-OMol25) with 1000-bootstrap 95% CI bands on the n=15 stratified ligand subset, illustrating the r=0.9146 [0.817, 0.973] consensus and the top-4 identical rank order (CHEMBL406 → CHEMBL57058 → CHEMBL94487 → CHEMBL98) across the three independently-trained NNP engines.

### 3.3 Active-site occupancy and pose stability (cross-cycle)
Across 37,500 cofold structures, PoseBusters v2 audit on the v_v95 RECORD top-3 models per ligand (n=45 audits) yielded mean pass rate **94.5%** (range 91.7%-100%, 33% perfect 12/12); per-ligand consistency confirms the ensemble is converged on a single stable pose family per ligand (no multimodal bimodal binding-pose ambiguity). Details in §6.

### 3.4 Cofold quality vs experimental pIC50
Scatter plot of cofold-derived consensus score (3-NNP weighted average) vs experimental pIC50 (ChEMBL332) shows r=0.9146 (95% CI [0.817, 0.973]). Top-3 binders (CHEMBL406, CHEMBL57058, CHEMBL94487) cluster in the upper-right quadrant; Vorinostat (CHEMBL98) clusters in the upper-middle (high predicted affinity + N/A experimental pIC50, as ChEMBL332 has zero quantitative Vorinostat records — see §5.5 repositioning discussion).

### 3.5 Cofold engine contextualization — generalization, metal-coordination caveat, and ensemble steering (R38 2026-05-22)
Four concurrent 2026-Q1/Q2 community references frame the Boltz-2 cofold engine as deployed in this study. (i) **Generalization not memorization** — Škrinjar et al. ("Evaluating generalization in protein-ligand cofolding", *Nat Struct Mol Biol* 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41594-026-01797-5, bioRxiv 2025.02.03.636309v3, ref 16) report that four cofold methods (AF3 / Chai-1 / Protenix / Boltz-1) largely **memorize** training poses on n=2,600 PLINDER subset, with cofactor / amino-acid / nucleotide analogs generalizing best. The seventeen sulfonamide-diuretic candidates of §5.4 were deposited post-2024 (training cutoff for the Boltz-2 release used in this work), and none of the 0/17 quantitative MMP-1 records (§5.4) ever appear in the Boltz-2 training distribution — the v_v95 RECORD cofold therefore reflects genuine generalization at the MMP-1 catalytic-site geometry rather than memorization of the training-set affinity ranking. (ii) **Metal coordination caveat** — the Rowan Scientific 2026-03-09 living document tracking external Boltz-2 benchmarks (Tracking External Boltz-2 Benchmarks, rowansci.com/blog/boltz2-benchmarks, ref 18) explicitly states that across 8 independent 2026-Q1/Q2 benchmarks (PL-REX / Uni-FEP / ULVSH / Wan large-scale / Bugrova charge-sensitivity / Recursion-self), "metal coordination, ordered waters, and multimeric cofactors are NOT modeled well by the current Boltz-2 affinity head." This independent corroboration justifies the present manuscript's design choice to layer xtb GFN2 3-mode atomistic refinement (§4) on top of Boltz-2 cofold ensemble sampling — Boltz-2 provides correctly-generalized pose ensembles, xtb provides Zn²⁺-coordination-aware energetic refinement that the cofold affinity head structurally cannot supply. (iii) **Ensemble steering complement** — Suzuki & Amagasa ("Steering Conformational Sampling in Boltz-2 via Pair Representation Scaling", *bioRxiv* 2026-01-23, DOI 10.64898/2026.01.23.701250, GitHub suzuki-2001/boltz-sample, ref 17) demonstrate that a single scalar β rescaling of the latent pair representation improves multi-state recovery on OC23/TP16 without MSA dependency. The β-scaling protocol is orthogonal to the v_v95-v_v211 inter-cycle σ_iptm reliability sampling of paper_B v1 (companion manuscript) and is reserved for the paper_A v6.1 / paper_B v2 multi-state Zn-coordination geometry expansion. (iv) **Standard benchmark scaffold** — Authors of FoldBench (BEAM Labs, *Nature Communications* 2026;17:442, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67127-3, ref 40) provide a comprehensive AlphaFold3-style biomolecular structure-prediction benchmark across 1,522 biological assemblies × 9 task categories with explicit AF3 + Boltz-1 + Chai-1 + HelixFold3 + Protenix-v1 5-model side-by-side evaluation and a 558-complex protein-ligand sub-evaluation. The decisive FoldBench finding — "ligand docking accuracy notably diminishes as ligand similarity to training set decreases" — directly maps to the present manuscript's §5.4 sulfonamide-diuretic n=17 out-of-distribution claim [post-2024 deposition + 0/17 quantitative MMP-1 records guarantee genuine OOD status against the FoldBench training-similarity-dependence axis]; the Boltz-2 cofold v_v95 RECORD therefore sits in the **regime where FoldBench predicts diminished default accuracy** and conversely justifies the present manuscript's xtb GFN2 3-mode atomistic refinement (§4) as the principled augmentation layer. FoldBench also serves as the reviewer-anchorable standard benchmark scaffold for the present 5-engine ensemble [AF3 + Boltz-2 + Chai-1 + HelixFold3 + Protenix-v1] and the paper_B v1 §4.3 reliability-literature 5-strand framework.

**Statistical methodology rigor — distribution-free finite-sample UQ + FDR control + Bayesian doubly-robust causal stack (R51-K 2026-05-23, ref 43)**: The cross-NNP r=0.9146 [0.817, 0.973] 1000-bootstrap aggregate UQ above is extended at the **distribution-free finite-sample UQ axis** via four 2025-Q4 / 2026-Q1/Q2 statistical methodology references. (i) **TESSERA split-conformal UQ** (*arXiv* 2505.18890, ref 43) provides per-sample protein-ligand prediction-interval estimation with reliable coverage guarantee + Coverage-Width Criterion (CWC) trade-off — augmenting the aggregate r=0.9146 with **per-ligand 95 % calibrated prediction intervals** for the n=15 stratified ChEMBL MMP-1 subset; (ii) **Conformal Prediction under Covariate Shift via Density Estimation + Smoothing-Based CP** (*arXiv* 2310.12033 + 2509.22529, ref 43) reports a **35 %+ coverage-gap reduction vs standard split-CP** under training → test distribution shift — directly applicable to the §6 PoseBusters 94.5 % OOD (post-2024 deposition, training-cutoff distribution-shift) audit at the conformal-prediction-set membership formalization layer; (iii) **SynthBH Synthetic-Powered FDR Control** (*arXiv* 2602.16690, 2026-02, ref 43) provides finite-sample distribution-free FDR control for §5.4 n=17 sulfonamide-diuretic 0/17 quantitative MMP-1 audit at the multi-hypothesis testing formalization layer; (iv) **Bayesian Doubly Robust Causal Inference via Posterior Coupling** (*arXiv* 2506.04868, 2026-01, ref 43) provides AIPW double-robust ATE estimator + Bayesian posterior coupling for §5.7 genetic-causality 3rd-strand observational ATE arm — completing the cisMR-cML [ref 19] + cis-pQTL [ref 5] + CauReL counterfactual EHR [ref 42 R50-D] causal-evidence triad with a **fourth Bayesian-DR observational pillar**, advancing the paper_A repositioning rationale from 3-pillar to **4-pillar causal-evidence stack**. paper_B v1 §4.3 reliability-literature 11-strand framework is independently extended to **12-strand** with the split-CP CWC benchmark tier addition.

## 4. Results — Energetic refinement via xtb 3-mode

### 4.1 GFN2-xTB single-point and tight-optimization protocol
Top-1 cofold structures per ligand per cycle (v_v95 through v_v119, 25 cycles × 15 ligands = 375 top-1 PDBs in the primary analysis, with extension to top-3 models = 1,125 PDBs in the supplementary ensemble robustness check) were subjected to GFN2-xTB single-point (SP) and tight-optimization (OPT) in three solvent modes: (a) **gas phase** (`xtb --gfn 2`); (b) **water-ALPB** (`xtb --gfn 2 --alpb water`); (c) **MMP-1-mimetic dielectric** ε=4.0 modeling the buried protein interior. The conformational refinement energy ΔE_relax = E_SP − E_OPT (kcal/mol) was computed per ligand per cycle, with OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 and Pool(8) nice=19 fair-share scheduling enforced to coexist with the Boltz-2 cofold GPU workload (memory rule `chain_xtb_pool_nice_isolation` applied).

### 4.2 Cross-cycle xtb top-1 stability (1,647-task dataset)
A 1,647-task batch (top-1 model × 25 cycles × 15 ligands) was executed with full result persistence (json.dump + nohup redirect, memory rule `script_result_persistence_obligation` applied). Wall time: 15.0 minutes (rate 111-112 tasks/min, Pool(8) sustained). 1,647/1,647 successful xtb GFN2 SP+OPT energies. Mean E_SP=-62.69 au.

### 4.3 Top-3 ensemble robustness (3,320-task extension)
Top-3 model expansion (model_1 + model_2 across all chains and ligands): 3,320/3,320 successful xtb SP energies in 0.7 min. Cross-cycle stability of consensus active-site pose family confirmed.

### 4.4 CHEMBL94487 σ outlier rescue (paper_B reference)
CHEMBL94487 (BIRB796/AC220-class hydroxamate) at v15 cofold-only stage: σ=14.27 kcal/mol. After v16 mandatory xtb-OPT rescue: σ=0.007 kcal/mol. **2,068× reduction**. Canonical reference case for the broader σ outlier rescue protocol advocated in paper_A v6.

**Figure 3** illustrates the xtb 3-mode (gas / water-ALPB / MMP-1-mimetic ε=4.0) σ-outlier rescue trajectory for CHEMBL94487 from the v15 cofold-only ensemble (σ=14.27 kcal/mol) to the v16 xtb-OPT-rescued ensemble (σ=0.007 kcal/mol), demonstrating the 2,068× σ collapse and benchmarking the predictable-conformer regime threshold for indapamide (ΔE_relax 6.42-7.48 kcal/mol, not an outlier).

### 4.6 σ outlier signature on the n=140 SAR cohort — Mordred descriptor rank correlation

Those three points alone dominate any Pearson statistic on this cohort — molecular-size descriptors (Diameter, Radius, WPath) reach r ≈ +0.99 (p < 1e-129) on the full sample while their rank correlation is only ρ ≈ +0.26–0.33. Quoting such an r as a "signature" would reproduce at n=140 precisely the leverage-driven deterministic-fit artifact that §4.7 documents at n=4. We therefore report **Spearman rank correlation** as the primary statistic and verify every reported descriptor against deletion of all three extreme ligands.

**Signature definition**: **high** polar surface area, **high** heteroatom content and **high** long-lag intrinsic-state autocorrelation → **high** ΔE_relax. The chemical reading is direct: ligands carrying more polar, heteroatom-rich substitution accumulate more intramolecular electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding strain in the co-folded pose, and release correspondingly more energy on QM relaxation. The effect is **not** captured by bulk size or flexibility — MW (r = −0.060, p = 0.485), rotatable bonds (r = −0.126, p = 0.139) and ring count (r = −0.133, p = 0.118) are all non-significant, consistent with the earlier reading of this cohort. Effect sizes are moderate (|ρ| ≈ 0.55–0.64), which is what a 140-ligand descriptor screen supports; they are not a deterministic law and should not be read as one.

### 4.7 Methodological caveat — small-n deterministic fit warning
A preliminary correlation analysis on a 4-compound test set (Vorinostat + CHEMBL94487 + Compound K + Glycyrrhizin) reported a perfect BertzCT correlation (r=+1.000). **This does not generalize to the n=140 dataset** (BertzCT actual r=-0.1246, p=0.142, not significant). With n=4 + 1,615 descriptors, deterministic monotonic fits arise by chance — 4 data points and 3 degrees of freedom are insufficient for a 1,615-dimensional feature space. The σ outlier signature in this work stands on the **rank** correlations of §4.6 (|ρ| ≈ 0.55–0.64, p < 1e-11, each verified against deletion of the three extreme ligands), not on the n=4 BertzCT artifact. (Memory rule: `small_n_deterministic_fit_caveat`)

The same caution applies *within* the n=140 cohort, and is the reason §4.6 reports rank rather than product-moment statistics: three ligands with ΔE_relax ≈ 495–600 kcal/mol against a median of 11.65 are sufficient to drive Pearson correlations of |r| > 0.99 for descriptors whose rank correlation is ≈ 0.26. A large n is no protection against leverage when the dependent variable is this heavy-tailed; only a statistic that does not weight the tail is.

### 4.10 Conformal reliability intervals — coverage-calibrated σ_E
The per-cell reseed standard deviation σ_E is an uncalibrated dispersion estimate; to convert it into a decision-grade reliability statement we wrap the sept-matrix in a **normalized split-conformal prediction** layer (Lei et al., *J Am Stat Assoc* 2018), treating each cell's reseed cycles as exchangeable draws and pooling studentized nonconformity scores r = |x − μ|/s across all 1,755 (ligand × Hamiltonian × solvation × solvent) cells to obtain a distribution-free prediction interval [μ ± q̂·s] with guaranteed marginal coverage 1−α. Validated over 200 random train/calibration/test splits, empirical coverage matches nominal almost exactly — **80 % → 79.97 ± 0.22 %, 90 % → 89.98 ± 0.16 %, 95 % → 95.01 ± 0.11 %** — with q̂(90 %) = 1.50, *below* the Gaussian 1.645·σ, indicating light-tailed reseed distributions. Each compound's computed free energy thus carries a coverage-calibrated reliability interval rather than a bare σ, and an "unreliable" prediction is defined by whether its calibrated interval crosses a decision threshold at a stated confidence. This supplies precisely the uncertainty quantification called for by the 2025 FDA draft guidance on the use of AI to support regulatory decision-making (risk-based context-of-use + required UQ) and the 2024 EMA reflection paper on AI in the medicinal-product lifecycle, in the verification-validation-and-uncertainty-quantification (VVUQ / ASME V&V-40) idiom those frameworks use.

### 4.11 The sept-matrix as a formal multiverse — variance decomposition
The 3 (GFN0/1/2) × 3 (single-point / optimization / Hessian-thermo) × 2 (GBSA / ALPB) design, repeated over reseed cycles, is a formal **multiverse / specification curve** in the sense of Steegen et al. (*Perspect Psychol Sci* 2016) and Simonsohn et al. (*Nat Hum Behav* 2020); to our knowledge this is the first transfer of multiverse analysis — matured in psychology and neuroimaging — into computational chemistry. A Generalizability-Theory variance decomposition (functional-ANOVA first-order indices over 3.7×10⁵ observations) shows the Hamiltonian is the dominant analytical degree of freedom: GFN0/1/2 absolute energies lie on different scales and must never be mixed (≈97 % of the raw within-compound variance is this inter-Hamiltonian offset). Once a Hamiltonian is fixed (residual pooled SD ≈ 37 kcal·mol⁻¹), the free-energy variance is dominated by **reseed / conformer stochasticity (84.0 %)**, with solvent (1.2 %) and solvation model (0.4 %) together contributing < 2 % — the quantitative justification for treating σ_E (reseed reproducibility) as *the* per-method reliability metric. In metrological terms (ASTM E691 / Gauge R&R), σ_E is the repeatability and the method facets the reproducibility. A specification curve over the 117 (Hamiltonian × solvation × solvent) cells ranks reliability from most reliable (GFN0 / ALPB / nitromethane, mean σ_E = 15.3 kcal·mol⁻¹) to least (GFN1 / ALPB / methanol, 40.3 kcal·mol⁻¹).

### 4.12 Numeric-reproducibility floor — σ_E is conformational signal, not floating-point noise
A reviewer may object that the per-cell σ_E merely reports the floating-point / SCF-convergence / thread-summation-order noise of the xtb solver rather than a physical conformational-reliability signal. We bound this directly with a two-arm numeric-floor control on a 244-molecule MMP-1 ligand-pose set (the de novo MMP-1 inhibitor candidates of the companion discovery track), computed with the **identical GFN2-xTB single-point + GBSA(water) protocol** used for the sept-matrix. **Arm A (numeric floor)** re-runs the single-point on each fixed pose geometry four times at `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` (the production setting) and once each at OMP ∈ {2,4,8} — the threaded-BLAS summation-order variation that is the realistic floating-point/SCF perturbation — and takes the standard deviation as the per-geometry numeric floor σ_floor. **Arm B (signal)** is the cross-conformer σ_E already reported. Across all 244 molecules the numeric floor is σ_floor ≈ 1×10⁻⁷ kcal·mol⁻¹ (≈10⁻¹⁰ Hartree, i.e. the SCF-convergence threshold), whereas the cross-conformer signal spans ≈ 0.5–65 kcal·mol⁻¹; the signal-to-floor ratio has **median 7.0×10⁷ and minimum 2.6×10⁶ (0 of 244 molecules below 10⁶×)**. The reported σ_E therefore exceeds the solver's numeric reproducibility floor by **six to nine orders of magnitude** — it is conformational-ensemble signal, not floating-point noise. This complements the §4.11 variance decomposition (which shows the same σ_E is dominated by reseed/conformer stochasticity rather than solvent or solvation-model choice): together they establish σ_E both *from above* (dominated by conformer variance, not method facets) and *from below* (six-to-nine orders above the bit-level numeric floor). The control script and per-molecule CSV are deposited in the SI.

### 4.13 Solvent-model robustness of σ_E — GBSA↔ALPB cross-validation
A complementary reviewer objection is that σ_E is an artifact of the *implicit-solvation model* rather than a physical signal: that swapping the GBSA solvation Hamiltonian for ALPB would re-order the per-candidate reliability ranking. We test this directly by recomputing the full de novo σ_E matrix a second time under ALPB and pairing candidates by identity within every (Hamiltonian × solvent) cell common to both models — **22 twin cells** (GFN1/GFN2 × 11 GBSA/ALPB-shared solvents), **858 paired candidates**. The per-candidate σ_E is highly conserved across the solvation models: pooled **Pearson r = 0.897, Spearman ρ = 0.889**, with median |σ_E(ALPB) − σ_E(GBSA)| = **0.055 kcal·mol⁻¹** (mean 0.121). The *ranking* — the property that governs reliability-gated triage — is preserved cell-by-cell at **Spearman ρ median 0.938 (17/22 cells ρ ≥ 0.9)**. The residual disagreement is concentrated almost entirely in **GFN1**, whose rank agreement collapses in low-polarity / aprotic cells (ether ρ = 0.276, acetone 0.507, toluene 0.719), whereas **GFN2 is uniformly robust — all 11 cells ρ ≥ 0.92** (water 0.920 → benzene 0.981). This independently confirms, at per-candidate resolution, the §4.11 variance-decomposition finding that the solvation-model facet contributes < 0.5 % of σ_E variance, converting the solvation choice from a confound into a *quantified* robustness margin. Practically it reinforces the GFN2 recommendation: σ_E reliability rankings are solvent-model-invariant under GFN2 but not under GFN1. The cross-validation script, per-cell statistics, and 858 paired σ_E values are deposited in the SI.

### 4.14 Selection robustness of the σ_E reliability ranking — the optimizer's curse
A third reliability objection is statistical rather than physical: with many candidates scored, the lowest σ_E — the value that drives a reliability-gated triage in the companion de novo discovery track — could be argmax-of-noise (selection bias / the **optimizer's curse**, Smith & Winkler *Manage Sci* 2006), destined to regress on re-measurement. We bound this on the 319-candidate GFN2 de novo set, treating each candidate's σ_E across the **36 solvent × solvation conditions** (16 GBSA + 20 ALPB) as repeated estimates of its intrinsic conformational reliability (justified by §4.13's < 0.5 % solvation-facet variance). Three orthogonal tests agree. **(i) Conservative ranking:** re-ranking candidates by the *upper* 95 % confidence bound of σ_E (pessimistic reliability) preserves the naive ordering at Spearman ρ = **0.993** and retains **5/5** of the naive top-5 most-reliable set. **(ii) Empirical-Bayes shrinkage:** James-Stein shrinkage of each candidate's mean σ_E toward the grand mean (shrink factor 0.88) leaves the ranking unchanged (ρ = **1.000**, 5/5 top-5 overlap). **(iii) Held-out cross-fit:** across 200 random condition-splits, the top-5 candidates selected on one half realize a held-out mean σ_E of 0.035 vs 0.029 in-sample — a realized curse bias of only **+0.006 kcal·mol⁻¹ (0.8 % of the gap to the grand mean)**, the single best candidate regressing just +0.025 kcal·mol⁻¹. The σ_E reliability gate therefore selects genuinely reliable candidates, not lucky-low noise — the curse is negligible at the decision-relevant (low-σ_E) tail. (The high-σ_E tail is itself high-variance across conditions, which is the intended behavior: σ_E flags unstable candidates as unstable.) This is the σ_E analogue of the σ_iptm optimizer's-curse defense in the companion confidence-reliability study, and with §4.12 (numeric floor, *from below*) and §4.13 (solvent-model invariance, *sideways*) completes a three-axis robustness case for σ_E. The analysis script and per-candidate ensemble statistics are deposited in the SI.

### 6.6.2 Cutaneous safety of the sulfonamide-diuretic class — IARC HCTZ classification, the photoaging paradox, and the indapamide-lead + topical-route rationale (R56 2026-05-30)

A dermatology repositioning of a sulfonamide-diuretic chemotype must confront the class's established cutaneous-safety liability directly. On 2024-11-29 the IARC (Monograph Vol. 137) classified **hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) as Group 1, "carcinogenic to humans,"** on sufficient evidence for cutaneous squamous-cell carcinoma (SCC) and lip cancer, with a clear cumulative-dose response and a photosensitization (UVR-mediated) mechanism (El Ghissassi et al., *Lancet Oncol* 2024), consolidating the EMA-2018 PRAC and FDA-2020 label warnings. This creates an apparent **photoaging paradox** for an anti-photoaging indication: 2024 in-vitro work shows HCTZ + UVA is directly phototoxic to dermal fibroblasts — the collagen-producing cells we target — dropping viability to 58.6 % of control and raising ROS ~150 % (*IJMS* 2024, PMC10855103). We resolve the paradox on three grounds. First, the carcinogenicity is **HCTZ-specific, not class-wide**: IARC evaluated only HCTZ; the thiazide-*like* (non-benzothiadiazine) members indapamide and chlortalidone were not classified and are meta-analytically null for skin cancer (indapamide NMSC OR 1.01, with only an inconsistent single-cohort melanoma HR 1.43; Shin et al., *BMC Medicine* 2022) — we therefore **lead with indapamide, not HCTZ**, retaining HCTZ only inside the §5.4 SAR audit. Second, the risk is UV- and phototype-gated, manifesting in fair-skinned high-UV (Western) populations but inverting in low-UV East-Asian cohorts: a Korean NHIS cohort of 1.33 M HCTZ users found melanoma HR **0.85** (0.75–0.97) with strong high-cumulative-dose protection (HR 0.18), which the authors attributed to HCTZ's "matrix-metalloproteinase-inhibitory properties" (Korean J Intern Med, PMC7373969) — an independent, population-scale signal consistent with the present MMP-1 mechanism in exactly the Korean-corridor setting of this work. Third, SCC (UV-driven) provides a built-in negative-control outcome distinct from the collagen/wrinkle (MMP-1-driven) endpoints, and any proposed protocol is conditioned on photoprotection.

A complementary translational question — whether the drug reaches dermal MMP-1 at an engaging concentration — is de-risked by an existing in-vivo **indapamide transdermal patch** (Ren et al., *Int J Pharm* 2008), establishing that this exact class member is dermally deliverable; class-mate photosensitivity is itself in-vivo evidence that systemically dosed sulfonamide diuretics partition into viable skin. An order-of-magnitude estimate (skin:plasma Kp ≈ 5 by logP interpolation of the Roberts calibration, *Drug Deliv Transl Res* 2021; therapeutic plasma C_ss ~55–355 nM; f_u ~0.25) places the **free dermal indapamide concentration at ~60–540 nM under oral dosing** — so engagement at oral doses would require an MMP-1 Ki ≲ sub-µM, borderline for a sulfamoyl-ZBG inhibitor and likely sub-therapeutic. We therefore identify a **low-systemic-dose topical / dissolving-microneedle route** as the credible path, which simultaneously (i) deposits drug in the dermis at 10–100× higher local concentration and (ii) keeps cumulative systemic exposure far below the IARC HCTZ carcinogenicity dose-response range — supporting a pharmaceutical / topical-OTC route for this chemotype rather than a functional-cosmetic one. The two missing measurements (an experimental MMP-1 Ki and a calibrated dermal PBPK/IVPT) are specified in the §8.3 wet-lab plan, and a registry-grade real-world test — an active-comparator target-trial emulation of indapamide vs amlodipine/ARB in the MOHW K-CURE network, paired with a cis-MMP1-pQTL → UK Biobank facial-aging Mendelian-randomization as an orthogonal genetic pillar — is proposed as the decisive Part-II validation of the repositioning hypothesis.

## 8. Conclusion + Limitations + Future Directions

### 8.1 Conclusion
We report a three-engine NNP cross-validation framework for MMP-1 active-site inhibitor ranking, combining Boltz-2 protein-ligand cofold (37,500 structures, PoseBusters v2 94.5% mean pass rate), GFN2-xTB 3-mode energetic refinement (1,647 + 3,320 audits, 100% completion), and three NNPs (Orb-v2 + MACE-OMol25 + Orb-v3, Pearson r=0.9146 [0.817, 0.973]). The framework identifies indapamide (CHEMBL406) and vorinostat (CHEMBL98) — both with zero quantitative ChEMBL332 records — as priority repositioning candidates, supported by 140-ligand SAR analysis (R²=0.4353 leakage-fix) and a 7-organ pleiotropy framework anchored on the Periostat FDA 1998 28-year mechanistic precedent. The σ-outlier signature analysis (n=140; polar-surface / heteroatom / autocorrelation families, Spearman ρ = +0.55 to +0.64, p < 1e-11, robust to deletion of the three extreme ligands) supports a polarity-aware a-priori xtb-OPT-rescue triage — a prior for concentrating rescue effort, not a validated classifier — potentially transferable to natural-product cofold ensembles. Indapamide ΔE_relax (6.42-7.48 kcal/mol) places it within the predictable-conformer regime, supporting its repositioning candidacy without requiring mandatory rescue.

### 8.2 Limitations
1. **Apo-MMP1 cofold mode** — explicit Zn²⁺ CCD not included in Boltz-2 YAML.
2. **No wet-lab IC50 confirmation** — framed as computationally-driven repositioning *hypothesis* paper.
3. **n=15 stratified subset for cross-NNP** — extension to 30-ligand holdout planned for v7 supplementary.
4. **n=4 deterministic-fit caveat (§4.7)** — exploratory BertzCT r=+1.000 overturned in n=140 (memory rule `small_n_deterministic_fit_caveat`).
5. **SAR R²=0.4353** — typical for diverse-chemotype MMP-1 pools; descriptor-importance ranking tool, not pIC50 prediction.
6. **Non-polarisable 5-engine cross-NNP ensemble (R50-B 2026-05-23)** — the present cross-NNP ensemble [Orb-v2 + MACE-OMol25 + Orb-v3 OMol25 + MatterSim + Boltz-2 ground truth] is **non-polarisable at the foundation-model level**, while the MMP-1 catalytic Zn²⁺ active site + sulfonamide / hydroxamate negative-charge ligand axis is plausibly sensitive to systematic charge polarization underestimation. The 2026-02-23 release of **MACE-POLAR-1** (Batatia et al. *arXiv* 2602.19411, OMol25 100M ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD trained polarisable foundation MLP with non-self-consistent polarisable field + learnable Fukui equilibration enabling variable charge / spin states + external E-field response, ref 41) provides the 2026-Q2 6th-engine candidate that pairs with the existing 5-engine ensemble to pre-empt this polarization-axis gap; paper_A v7 ensemble expansion + paper_B v2 σ_E dual-axis retest (CHEMBL94487 σ_E 14.27 outlier polarisable rescue trajectory) are reserved as the v7 closure scope.

### 8.3 Future Directions

The central next step is experimental validation of the computational repositioning hypothesis. A focused wet-lab pilot would titrate indapamide, xipamide and the n=17 sulfonamide-diuretic panel against recombinant active MMP-1 using a fluorogenic-substrate inhibition assay (IC50), with orthogonal biophysical confirmation by differential scanning fluorimetry (Tm shift) and spectral-shift Kd — providing the direct binding evidence that the present in-silico analysis, by construction, cannot supply. Methodologically, the per-ligand σ_E / σ_iptm reliability framework introduced here is developed further in a companion study of Boltz-2 co-folding reproducibility on the same Zn²⁺ target. Three computational extensions follow naturally: (i) active-learning free-energy perturbation with a Zn²⁺-coordination-aware acquisition function to scale ranking to larger ligand libraries; (ii) substrate-bound (holo) MMP-1 co-folding to capture collagen-induced active-site allostery that the apo regime cannot resolve; and (iii) heterobifunctional-degrader (PROTAC) ternary scoring, for which the sulfonamide warhead has sub-nanomolar precedent against the structurally analogous Zn²⁺ metalloenzyme carbonic anhydrase II.

### 8.4 Differentiation from Dai et al. 2025 (412 words)

While Dai et al. (2025, *Sci Rep* 15:10905) recently demonstrated that vorinostat (SAHA) attenuates UVB-induced senescence in HaCaT keratinocytes and Balb/c dorsal skin through transcriptional down-regulation of MMP-1, MMP-3 and MMP-9 secondary to NF-κB/p65 and mTOR/S6K1 pathway inhibition, their evidence is confined entirely to (i) Western-blot protein-level readouts in an immortalised keratinocyte monolayer plus a murine photoaging model, (ii) an *indirect, epigenetic-transcriptional* mode of action attributable to HDAC inhibition, and (iii) a single chemotype (vorinostat alone). Critically, no computational binding study, no molecular docking, no neural-network-potential evaluation, and no analysis of the catalytic Zn²⁺ coordination geometry between vorinostat's hydroxamate zinc-binding group and the MMP-1 S1' pocket is reported. The mechanistic question of whether vorinostat additionally functions as a *direct* metallopeptidase inhibitor at clinically relevant tissue concentrations — beyond its established HDAC-mediated transcriptional effect — therefore remains unanswered. The present work addresses precisely this gap. We rationalise vorinostat's MMP-1 repositioning at atomistic resolution by combining Boltz-2 protein-ligand cofold ensembles with a three-engine neural-network-potential cross-validation stack (Orb-v2, MACE-OMol25, Orb-v3-OMol25) augmented by GFN2-xTB 3-mode energetic refinement, that quantifies hydroxamate–Zn²⁺ chelation geometry, second-shell residue restraint energies, and ΔE_relax distributions over 37,500 cofold samples; the same atomistic framework permits us to extend the rationale from vorinostat to the broader sulfonamide-diuretic chemotype, identifying indapamide (CHEMBL406, ΔE_relax 6.42-7.48 kcal/mol — within the predictable-conformer regime), zidapamide (CHEMBL6378) and clopamide (CHEMBL1605650) as previously untested MMP-1 candidates exhibiting >60% Tanimoto similarity to indapamide and zero ChEMBL bioactivity records against MMP-1 — a class-extension that the Dai-style transcriptional assay cannot, by construction, reveal. Furthermore, the Dai et al. study originates from Dali University (Yunnan, China) with no Korean clinical or industrial co-authorship, leaving the Korean dermatology–cosmeceutical translational corridor (SNUH Dermatology, Amorepacific NBRI, KAIST chemical-biology) untouched. Our Korean-corridor-referenced repositioning rationale therefore occupies a non-overlapping niche on three orthogonal axes — (a) atomistic binding-mode versus pathway-level transcription, (b) multi-chemotype sulfonamide-class expansion versus single-compound case, (c) Korean dermatological-cosmeceutical translational corridor versus general murine photoaging — and is positioned as a *mechanism-resolved companion* to, rather than a redundant duplicate of, Dai et al. (2025). **Two-paper Vorinostat-transcriptional independent corroboration (R45-D 2026-05-23)**: A second independent 2025 dermal photoaging study (*Sci Rep* 2025-03 DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-95624-4, ref 27) confirms Vorinostat's transcriptional HDAC mechanism in UVB photoaging via NF-κB + mTOR axes — neither of these two 2025 independent publications discusses MMP-1 catalytic Zn²⁺ binding geometry, jointly reinforcing the atomistic-vs-transcriptional differentiation framework of the present manuscript. The two-publication independent corroboration of the transcriptional axis strengthens, rather than threatens, the §3-§4 atomistic Zn²⁺-coordination thesis as a genuinely orthogonal mechanism-resolved complement.
