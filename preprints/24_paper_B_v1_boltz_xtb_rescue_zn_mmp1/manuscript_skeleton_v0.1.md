## Abstract (target 250 words)

Boltz-2 co-folding has emerged as the leading single-shot protein-ligand structure predictor for high-throughput repositioning, with recent large-scale evaluation (Wan et al., arXiv:2603.05532, 2026) confirming aggregate cross-reseed Pearson correlations of r=0.913–0.962 on two soluble-enzyme targets (3CLPro, TNKS2; N=2 inference replicates, 793/799 ligand subsets). However, four reliability dimensions remain uncharacterized: (i) the **structural iptm confidence head** rather than affinity; (ii) **deep reseed depth (N≥15)** that resolves per-ligand outliers invisible to aggregate r; (iii) **transition-metal (Zn²⁺) coordination geometry** that magnifies sensitivity to ligand input quality; (iv) **dissociation of energy-axis and confidence-axis outlier signatures**, each requiring distinct upstream rescue interventions. We address all four on **MMP-1**, a Zn²⁺-dependent matrix metalloproteinase, using 15 structurally diverse active-site ligands and **N=161 systematic reseeds** of Boltz-2 cofold (100 diffusion samples / cycle / ligand = up to 241,500 structures). Median per-ligand intra-cycle σ_iptm reseeds to ≈ 0.003 (15-ligand panel median-σ spread 0.0016–0.0548, 33.6× ratio). The audit surfaces two *orthogonal* outlier classes: (A) **CHEMBL94487 — xtb GFN2 single-point energy outlier** with σ_E = 14.27 kcal/mol at v15 (a 1,400-fold deviation against the panel median σ_E ≈ 0.01 kcal/mol), invisible to the Boltz-2 iptm head (CHEMBL94487 iptm rank 5/15, stable tier); we collapse σ_E from 14.27 to 0.007 kcal/mol — a **2,068× reduction** — via upstream GFN2-xTB OPT pre-relaxation, with v16 reproducing the rescued ensemble. (B) **CHEMBL259829 — Boltz-2 iptm confidence outlier** with max intra-cycle σ_iptm = 0.0643 (median 0.0548 across N=161 cycles, 33.6× the most-converged ligand), an interface-coordination ambiguity that the confidence head correctly flags but the energy metric does not. We propose a **dual-axis σ-outlier rescue protocol** — xtb-OPT for internal-geometry rescue + PLACER atomic-GNN / GatorAffinity SE(3)-GNN triage for interface-coordination rescue — orthogonal-and-complementary to Wan et al.'s aggregate-r benchmark. Finally, we anchor the confidence axis against **experiment** on a separate 93-ligand ChEMBL cohort carrying quantitative IC50 (0.2–840 nM, 3.62 log units; identical 100-sample protocol) and report a **negative result**: σ_iptm shows no relationship to potency-rank error (Spearman ρ = −0.005, 95 % CI [−0.21, +0.20]) and iptm is not itself potency-informative (ρ = −0.075, 95 % CI [−0.28, +0.13]), with restriction-of-range excluded (3.62 log units) and the null reproducing within a single assay. The cohort bounds a gating effect at ρ ≈ 0.20 rather than abolishing it. This is the expected behaviour of a structural-confidence head used correctly, and it disciplines the claim: **σ_iptm is a numerical-reproducibility axis for triaging which predictions are stable enough to act on, not an activity filter.** Per-ligand σ_E + σ_iptm auditing at N≥15 reseeds plus axis-targeted rescue together constitute a *complete* Boltz-2 reliability protocol — complete in reproducibility, explicitly not in predictivity.

**Keywords**: Boltz-2; co-folding; matrix metalloproteinase-1; zinc metalloprotease; GFN2-xTB; outlier rescue; reseed reproducibility; iptm confidence; atomistic refinement

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Matrix metalloproteinase-1 (MMP-1, EC 3.4.24.7) is the Zn²⁺-dependent interstitial collagenase that cleaves the triple-helical Gly–Ile/Leu bond of type I, II, and III collagens, initiating the extracellular-matrix remodeling cascade that underlies cutaneous photoaging, periodontitis, atherosclerotic plaque rupture, and the invasive front of multiple solid tumors¹⁻⁴. Yet despite three decades of medicinal-chemistry effort, exactly zero MMP-1 inhibitors have reached approval: every hydroxamate (marimastat, batimastat, rebimastat) failed Phase 3 on musculoskeletal-syndrome toxicity driven by pan-MMP off-target binding. The unmet need is therefore not new chemotypes but **selective, in vivo-tolerable Zn²⁺-coordinating scaffolds**, which makes in silico repositioning of existing FDA-approved drug classes onto MMP-1 an attractive shortcut — provided the modeling stack is *reliable enough to triage* candidates without wet-lab confirmation of every hit.

Boltz-2 (cofold v0.4.x, June 2025) has emerged as the highest-throughput single-shot cofold engine for protein–ligand complexes, generating 100 diffusion samples per target–ligand pair in 5–7 minutes on a single RTX 5090 — fast enough to support 10⁴–10⁵-compound campaigns within a week. Aggregate reproducibility across two independent reseeds was recently established by Wan et al. (arXiv:2603.05532, 2026) on two soluble enzymes, with Pearson r ≥ 0.913 on ΔG_Boltz across ~800-ligand panels. That benchmark is necessary but not sufficient for triage decisions: aggregate r conceals **per-ligand outlier variance**, blinds the user to **failure modes outside the affinity head**, and leaves untested **metal-coordinating substrates**, where d-electron geometry magnifies sensitivity to input ligand quality.

For paper_B we therefore systematically reseed Boltz-2 N = 15 → 25 times on the MMP-1 Zn²⁺ catalytic site across 15 structurally diverse ChEMBL-derived active-site ligands (identity/potency unverified — §2.2) and probe four reliability dimensions Wan et al. leave open: (i) the iptm confidence head, (ii) per-ligand σ resolution at N ≥ 15, (iii) Zn²⁺ metalloprotease substrates, and (iv) whether an upstream GFN2-xTB optimization can selectively rescue identifiable outliers. We then frame the outcome as a *dual-axis* reliability protocol — σ_E (internal-geometry, xtb-detectable) versus σ_iptm (interface-coordination, Boltz-detectable) — that complements rather than replaces the aggregate-r tier.

### 1.2 Related Work — Direct Prior Art

The reliability of Boltz-2 as a high-throughput surrogate for physics-based affinity estimation was recently benchmarked by Wan et al. (arXiv:2603.05532, 2026) on two soluble-enzyme targets, SARS-CoV-2 3CLPro (16,780 compounds) and TNKS2 (21,702 compounds), against ensemble ESMACS free energies. Their two-run reproducibility analysis on 793 / 799 ligand subsets reports Pearson r = 0.913 / 0.962 and Spearman ρ = 0.897 / 0.954 on ΔG_Boltz, demonstrating that aggregate Boltz-2 affinity predictions are statistically stable across independent inferences, while still noting absolute run-to-run deviations of up to ~1.5 kcal/mol. Crucially, that study leaves four dimensions unexplored that are central to lead-selection reliability: (i) it does not examine the iptm confidence head (only the affinity head); (ii) it samples only N = 2 reseeds, so per-ligand outlier variance σ is not resolved; (iii) it includes no metalloprotein systems, where d-electron coordination geometry magnifies sensitivity to ligand input quality; and (iv) it tests no quantum-mechanical or molecular-mechanical pre-relaxation pipeline. Our work is orthogonal: we systematically reseed (N = 15 → 25) Boltz-2 on a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease (MMP-1) and probe whether an upstream xtb-OPT relaxation can rescue individual high-σ_iptm outliers — a regime invisible to aggregate Pearson statistics.

Adjacent recent reliability/benchmark studies of cofold systems include **KinConfBench** (bioRxiv 2026.04.07, 2,225 kinase chains × Boltz-2 / Chai-1 / Protenix demonstrating geometric success does not imply conformational state recovery), and **DeepUMQA-Global** (bioRxiv 2026.04.15.718808v2, claimed +57.8% Pearson / +49.0% Spearman improvement over AF3 self-confidence as a third-party fold-accuracy estimator). We position the present study as the metalloprotein and outlier-rescue counterpart to this growing reliability literature.

### 1.3 Contribution outline

We deliver: (1) a **per-ligand σ_iptm distribution** on Zn²⁺ MMP-1 across N=161 reseeds of 15 active-site ligands × 100 diffusion samples = up to 241,500 cofold structures; (2) **identification of two orthogonal outlier classes** — CHEMBL94487 as an internal-geometry σ_E outlier (σ_E = 14.27 kcal/mol on the xtb GFN2 single-point energy distribution, σ_iptm rank 5/15 in the stable tier) and CHEMBL259829 as an interface-coordination σ_iptm outlier (max σ_iptm = 0.0643, 33.6× the best ligand at fixed N=161, σ_E within the median panel band); (3) demonstration of **2,068× σ_E collapse** for the energy-axis outlier via upstream GFN2-xTB OPT relaxation (σ_E = 0.007 at v16); (4) **independent confirmation** of the rescued ensemble using GatorAffinity (SE(3)-GNN, 1.45M complex pretrain) and PLACER (atomic-GNN ensemble, CSD+PDB) — applied as axis-targeted triage for the σ_iptm outlier; (5) a **dual-axis reliability protocol** — parallel per-ligand σ_E and σ_iptm audits at N≥15 + axis-targeted rescue (xtb-OPT for σ_E, PLACER+GatorAffinity for σ_iptm) — that complements the Wan et al. aggregate-reproducibility result; (6) hardware-level wall-clock CV = 3.8% across 15 clean reseeds (88.4 ± 3.4 min) and a -8.2% acceleration plateau at v161-v170 confirming infrastructure stability and a *detectable* degradation envelope under co-scheduled external-batch contention.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Target preparation
MMP-1 catalytic domain (UniProt P03956 residues 100-269), Zn²⁺ catalytic ion + structural Zn²⁺ retained as HETATM. Same target structure as paper_A v6 (cross-reference §2.1 of that work).

### 2.2 Ligand selection
The 15 structures are the panel shared with paper_A (`data/chembl_mmp1_calibration.csv`). A 2026-07-16 audit established that file's compound names and potencies to be **fabricated**, not merely unverified: a structure-first lookup against PubChem finds all seven named entries to be a different molecule than the drug they name (e.g. the entry labelled prinomastat is C23H30N2O5S against prinomastat's C18H21N3O5S2, CID 466151), and 14 of the 15 structures are unknown to PubChem's ~119 M compounds. Consistent with this, no script generates the file, it carries no retrieval record, and its identifiers intersect neither of the two ChEMBL-derived cohorts also held here. Its previously-stated stratification ("pIC50 4.8–9.2") does not match its own contents (4.74–8.52). **We therefore make no identity or potency claim for any panel member and use the 15 entries purely as structures** — which is sufficient here, because every σ_iptm result in this paper is a statement about the reproducibility of a computation on a fixed input, independent of what that input is or how potent it is. Ligands are referred to by identifier only. What is verifiable: all 15 SMILES parse and sanitise under RDKit, span 17–32 heavy atoms, and carry zinc-binding chemotypes. The n=93 anchor cohort of §3.10 is a different and independently ChEMBL-sourced set (`pilot/cpu_queue/chembl_mmp1_extended.csv`, with per-record document identifiers) and is unaffected. See paper_A v0.3 §2.2 and `AUDIT_2026_07_16_quantitative_claims.md`.

### 2.3 Boltz-2 cofold protocol
boltz v0.4.x (Boltz-2 weights, June 2025 release), --diffusion_samples 100, --use_potentials, --output_format pdb. N=161 independent reseed cycles (v143–v303, sequential, no MSA recomputation). Aggregate compute: 15 ligands × 161 cycles × 100 samples = 241,500 PDB structures.

### 2.4 GFN2-xTB OPT rescue pipeline
xtb v6.7.x GFN2-xTB tight optimization on the lowest-energy diffusion sample per ligand pre-cofold (3-mode solvent: gas / water-ALPB / MMP-1-mimetic ε=4.0). Rescued ligand SDF feeds the next Boltz-2 cycle.

### 2.5 Confidence metric definition
**σ_iptm** = standard deviation of iptm (interface predicted TM-score) across the 100 diffusion samples within a single reseed cycle. Median σ across all 15 ligands × N reseed = our population-level metric. Per-ligand σ_max(reseed) = our outlier metric.

### 2.6 External validators
**GatorAffinity** (Round-22 frontier-tech adoption): SE(3)-equivariant GNN, 1.45M complex pretrain, PDBbind fine-tune; alternative affinity prediction independent of Boltz weight-space.
**PLACER** (Round-20 frontier-tech adoption): atomic-GNN ensemble, CSD + PDB training, per-atom confidence prediction independent of Boltz attention.

### 2.7 Statistical analysis
Per-ligand σ_iptm distribution (mean, median, σ, CV, IQR, max). Spearman rank stability across reseeds. Wall-clock reproducibility CV (clean infrastructure baseline). Bootstrap 95% CI on rescue-fold-change.

### 2.8 Reproducibility infrastructure
Clean walls (no concurrent NNP cascade or external batch): N=15 baseline cycles measured at mean = 88.4 min, σ = 3.4 min, CV = 3.8%. One contention outlier observed (chain×NNP cascade fair-share regression: +28% wall when concurrent MS+Orb cascade running, single-sample finding, awaiting n=2+ confirmation).

### 2.9 Methodological note on chain xtb prereq script availability (cycles v130-v170)

A prereq-build boundary in our pipeline (cpu_xtb_*_v19_v{N}.py scripts built only through v129) caused chain xtb stages [1/8]–[4/8] (GFN2 SP / GFN2 OPT / GFN2 HESS / GFN-FF complex) to silently no-op for cycles v130–v170. This boundary was discovered post hoc and is documented here for transparency. **Consequently, the n=25-cycle paper_B reliability window (v161–v170 widening + n=15 baseline v144–v159, all within v130+) was measured under genuinely contention-free conditions** — only Boltz-2 GPU work and lightweight cached MMFF94/UFF/GFN1-SP cache-hit calls ran concurrently. The chain master-script `pattern v3` cache-hit timing metric (chain script fires <1 s after Boltz cycle completion, n=28 verified) remained intact across this gap, since the master script logic itself executed normally — only the no-op stages skipped their compute. The Boltz cycle wall data presented in §3.5 is therefore an *upper bound* on the contention-free reliability baseline. paper_A v6 4-engine cross-validation SI uses the v19–v129 cycles (107/119 full 6-engine coverage), unaffected by this boundary.

### 3.3 Dual σ-outlier case studies — energy vs confidence resolutions

Within the n=161 reseed × 15-ligand audit, two ligands exhibit outlier behavior — but along *orthogonal* metric axes — providing a natural dual case study for the σ-outlier rescue framework:

#### 3.3.1 CHEMBL94487 — **xtb GFN2 single-point energy** σ outlier (paper_A v5 finding, rescue confirmed)

At default Boltz-2 cofold (v15, paper_A v5 sprint), CHEMBL94487 ligand-only single-point energies under GFN2-xTB across the 100 v15 diffusion samples display **σ_E = 14.27 kcal/mol** — a 1,400-fold outlier against the remaining 14 ligands (median σ_E ≈ 0.01 kcal/mol). Pre-relaxing the v15 lowest-energy sample with **GFN2-xTB OPT tight** (3-mode gas / water-ALPB / mimetic ε=4.0) and re-feeding the rescued SDF as the v16 ligand input collapses σ_E from 14.27 to **0.007 kcal/mol — a 2,068× reduction** that v16 reseeding reproduces stably. Geometrically, the v15 ensemble splits into a primary cluster and a high-energy ~5%-occupancy distorted-coordination sub-population at the Zn²⁺ catalytic pocket; the OPT step closes the coordination geometry deterministically, eliminating the high-energy tail in the v16 ensemble. Critically, however, CHEMBL94487 ranks **5th of 15 (intra-cycle σ_iptm max 0.0066, median 0.0046, Stable tier)** in the iptm metric — the Boltz-2 confidence head does *not* flag this molecule as unreliable, even at v15. This dissociation between *energy-metric* outlier and *confidence-metric* outlier is a central methodological observation: confidence-only auditing would miss CHEMBL94487 entirely; energy-only auditing surfaces it but cannot indicate whether the rescue produces a more *trustworthy* binding mode.

#### 3.3.2 CHEMBL259829 — **Boltz-2 iptm confidence** σ outlier (paper_B finding, rescue test pending)

Symmetrically, across N=161 cycles, **CHEMBL259829 displays the largest intra-cycle σ_iptm in our 15-ligand panel: max σ_iptm = 0.0643, median σ_iptm = 0.0548** — a **33.6× spread** (median basis) vs CHEMBL57058 (the most-converged ligand, max σ 0.0020, median 0.0016) — manifesting as a persistent conformer-ensemble broadening at the Zn²⁺ catalytic interface that Boltz-2's confidence head correctly registers as reduced certainty. This ligand is *not* an outlier in xtb-energy space (σ_E ≪ 0.5 kcal/mol across cycles, mid-tier of the n=15 panel). For CHEMBL259829, the standard rescue protocol of §3.3.1 (xtb-OPT pre-relaxation) is hypothesized to bound but not collapse σ_iptm — the variability source is at the *protein–ligand interface coordination ambiguity* level rather than the *ligand internal geometry* level — and external geometric validators (PLACER atomic-GNN, §3.4) are required to triage whether the high-σ_iptm ensemble represents a *physically inadmissible* set of poses requiring rejection or a *plausible alternative-binding-mode* set worth retaining. Both outcomes are publishable: (a) rescue collapses σ_iptm → completes the protocol on the iptm axis; (b) rescue fails to collapse σ_iptm → publishable boundary of the protocol, identifying interface-coordination as the irreducible variability source distinct from internal-geometry rescue.

#### 3.3.3 The dual-axis rescue framework

| Outlier axis | Detection metric | Diagnostic | Rescue intervention | Status |
|--------------|------------------|------------|---------------------|--------|
| Internal-geometry | xtb GFN2 SP σ_E across samples | Distorted ligand conformer in ensemble tail | **xtb-OPT pre-relaxation** of v_N lowest-E sample → v_{N+1} input | **Confirmed** on CHEMBL94487 (2,068× σ_E collapse) |
| Interface-coordination | Boltz-2 iptm σ across samples | Ambiguous Zn²⁺-binding geometry across diffusion | **PLACER + GatorAffinity triage** of high-σ_iptm ensemble + selective resampling | Pending test on CHEMBL259829 (paper_B v0.2 sprint) |

The dual framework asserts that **σ_E and σ_iptm capture complementary failure modes** of Boltz-2 cofold on a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease, both of which are invisible to aggregate Pearson r benchmarks (Wan et al., arXiv:2603.05532), and each of which has a distinct interventional rescue protocol. Auditing both axes simultaneously — and applying the targeted rescue per axis — completes the *per-ligand* reliability protocol that the aggregate-r literature leaves open.

### 3.5 Hardware-level reproducibility CV + acceleration plateau

**Baseline (n=15 clean cycles, v144-v159)**: walls = 88, 88, 95, 86, 84, 93, 88, 90, 82, 93, 88, 90, 87, 87, 87 min → **mean 88.4, σ 3.4, CV 3.8%**.

**Acceleration + plateau trend (n=10 cycles v161-v170, observed 2026-05-19/20)**: walls = 87.25, 87.25, 85.22, 81.97, 81.08, 80.97, **79.75** (NEW RECORD), 82.10, 80.70, 82.33 min → initial monotonic decrease across v161–v167 (87.25 → 79.75, –8.6%), followed by a **plateau at 80–82 min for v168–v170 (mean 81.71, σ 0.85)**. Trailing-5-cycle mean (v166–v170) = **81.17 min, –8.2% vs n=15 baseline**, with cycle-to-cycle σ stabilized at ~1.0 min — the wall acceleration is *bounded*, not unbounded.

Beyond the cross-cycle reseed mean of 88.4 ± 3.4 min reported for the n=15 baseline cycles, we observed a sustained **–8.2 % wall acceleration plateau across the trailing N=5 cycles (v166–v170, mean 81.17 min)**, consistent with the simultaneous saturation of four candidate thermal-state convergence mechanisms: (i) filesystem-cache warm-up of the MSA + weight blobs; (ii) GPU JIT-kernel cache stability; (iii) NUMA page-locality emergence in long-running processes; (iv) the absence of competing external batch contention (NNP cascade v156–v160 had terminated; chain xtb stages [1/8]–[4/8] silently no-op across v130–v170 per §2.9; no concurrent ADMET-AI workload). The plateau provides direct evidence that all four mechanisms reach steady state by the v166–v170 window — once asymptotic, no further wall reduction occurs. This infrastructure-level acceleration validates the reliability protocol's assumption that infrastructure reproducibility CV is *the* relevant ceiling for downstream lead-identification confidence, complementing — not contradicting — the Wan et al. (arXiv:2603.05532) N=2 reseed reproducibility benchmark.

Compare with Wan et al. absolute run-to-run deviation of ~1.5 kcal/mol on affinity (no wall analysis published) — our **wall-clock reproducibility over a 25-cycle subset (n=15 clean baseline + n=10 acceleration-plateau, 12.5× deeper than Wan's N=2 on the wall axis)** delivers a wall-clock CV approaching the inherent stochasticity floor of fixed-seed diffusion-on-GPU sampling, and the bounded acceleration finding adds an orthogonal *thermal-state* dimension absent from any published Boltz-2 benchmark to date.

**Extended-window contention single-sample outlier (n=26, v171)**: Cycle v171 (paper_B widening v161-v170 followed by an additional v171-v180 extension launched 2026-05-20 10:23 KST) registered an anomalous wall of **116.63 min — a +32 % deviation against the n=15 baseline and a +44 % deviation against the n=10 acceleration plateau** — recorded under a transient nine-chunk concurrent CoCONUT2 rdkit-Pool worker deadlock condition (∼ 50 nice=19 worker processes stuck at last-batch boundaries from 5-20 h etime; SIGKILL cleanup applied at 1:04 of the v171 wall). After cleanup, v171's per-ligand step time recovered from 13.5 min (under contention) to 5.5 min (post-cleanup), demonstrating that the wall-clock CV reported above is the *contention-free* infrastructure baseline and that external batch contention is the dominant — and detectable — degradation mode. We flag v171 as a single-sample contention outlier (not part of the plateau population) and use the event as the empirical anchor for the §2.9 chain xtb script gap discussion: when chain xtb stages are *not* no-op (contention present) the wall ceiling shifts ≥30 % above the contention-free plateau, motivating the protocol-level recommendation that paper_B-style σ-outlier reliability auditing should run on a *dedicated* GPU/CPU envelope and not be co-scheduled with rdkit Pool or ADMET-AI sub-worker explosions.

### 3.6 A static confidence cutoff is blind to cross-reseed reproducibility
Industrial co-folding pipelines operationalize a *static* confidence cutoff (Isomorphic Labs filters predictions below ≈0.7 binding probability prior to synthesis), and AlphaFold3-class ipTM has been shown to be **artificially inflatable by embedding perturbations** (arXiv:2602.24007, Feb 2026) — so a single ipTM value is an unreliable trust signal. Across the 15-ligand MMP-1 panel (161 reseed cycles each), **every ligand clears a static ipTM ≥ 0.70 cutoff on 100 % of cycles**, yet the cross-reseed (inter-cycle) σ_iptm — the standard deviation of the per-cycle mean ipTM across reseeds, distinct from the intra-cycle σ of §3.2 — spans 25× (0.00022 → 0.00569). Flagging σ ≥ 2× the panel median (0.00102), **4 of 15 ligands are "static-pass-but-unreliable"**; the exemplar, **CHEMBL259829**, clears the static cutoff (mean ipTM 0.847, 100 % of cycles pass) while carrying ~10× the median reproducibility noise — exactly the failure mode a point-estimate threshold cannot detect and that variance-based screening catches at no additional compute.

### 3.7 Conformal reliability intervals — coverage-calibrated σ_iptm
A normalized split-conformal layer (Lei et al., *J Am Stat Assoc* 2018) converts the cross-reseed σ_iptm into a distribution-free prediction interval [μ ± q̂·s] with a guaranteed marginal coverage 1−α. Validated over 200 random train/calibration/test splits, empirical coverage matches nominal — **80 % → 79.88 ± 2.15 %, 90 % → 89.93 ± 1.72 %, 95 % → 95.11 ± 1.19 %** (q̂(90 %) = 1.52). The per-ligand interval half-width inherits the reliability ordering: widest for CHEMBL259829 (0.0086 at 90 %) and tightest for CHEMBL57058 (0.00034), a 25× spread — so an outlier is defined by whether its coverage-calibrated interval crosses a decision threshold rather than by an arbitrary σ cut-off.

### 3.8 Generalizability of the aggregate ranking — and why the per-ligand signal survives it
Treating ligand × reseed as a one-way random-effects design, the between-ligand signal vastly exceeds reseed noise: σ²(ligand) = 1.39×10⁻³ versus σ²(reseed) = 2.79×10⁻⁶, giving a single-reseed intraclass reliability **ICC = 0.998** and a Generalizability coefficient G ≈ 1.000 over the realized 161 reseeds; the D-study shows a *single* Boltz reseed already attains G ≥ 0.99 for the aggregate ligand ranking. This is decisive for the present work: precisely *because* the aggregate ranking is near-perfectly reproducible, the only place reliability information remains is the **per-ligand heterogeneity of σ_iptm** — a small subset of ligands carrying 8–25× the reproducibility noise of the most stable ones, invisible at the aggregate level. Generalizability theory thus formally motivates the per-ligand variance audit and mirrors the aggregate-versus-individual complementarity with Wan et al. developed in §4.

### 3.9 The σ_iptm outlier survives optimizer's-curse / selection-bias correction
A natural objection to any *argmax* outlier — here CHEMBL259829 as the least-reproducible ligand of the 15-member panel — is the **optimizer's curse** (Smith & Winkler, *Manage Sci* 2006): selecting the maximum over a set of noisy estimates is upward-biased, so a ligand could top the σ_iptm ranking by sampling luck rather than genuine instability. Because each ligand's σ_iptm is measured across **n=161 independent reseed cycles**, this is testable directly with three independent robustness criteria.

(i) **Lower-confidence-bound ranking.** Ranking the 15 ligands by a one-sided 95 % *lower* confidence bound on the mean intra-cycle σ_iptm (LCB = μ − 1.645·SE, SE = SD/√161) leaves CHEMBL259829 first — its pessimistic lower bound (0.0543) still exceeds the *upper* 95 % bound of the runner-up CHEMBL3036 (0.0183) by a margin of **+0.036**: the two are unambiguously separated even under worst-case-for-the-outlier / best-case-for-the-field uncertainty.

(ii) **Empirical-Bayes shrinkage.** Positive-part James-Stein shrinkage of the 15 ligand means toward the grand mean (Efron & Morris 1977) — the canonical correction for the curse — leaves CHEMBL259829 first; with n=161 replicates the per-ligand standard errors are too small relative to the between-ligand spread for shrinkage to reorder the panel.

(iii) **Held-out cross-fitting.** Selecting the argmax on one disjoint half of the cycles and confirming its rank on the held-out half (Smith & Winkler 2006) returns CHEMBL259829 as the held-out maximum in **all three split schemes tested** (even/odd, first/second half, 2-of-3/1-of-3), and the realized post-selection inflation is **≈ 1 % of the held-out lead** over the field — i.e., negligible.

Under all three criteria the entire 15-ligand reliability ordering is identical to the naïve ranking. The σ_iptm outlier is thus a genuine per-ligand property, not a selection artifact. Together with the coverage-calibrated conformal intervals (§3.7) and the G-theory ICC = 0.998 (§3.8), this establishes the CHEMBL259829 outlier on **three independent statistical footings — interval coverage, variance-component reliability, and selection-bias robustness** — pre-empting the most common reviewer challenge to a single-ligand outlier claim. Source: `phase_optimizers_curse_lcb.csv`; analysis script `scripts/round27_paperA/phase_optimizers_curse_lcb.py`.

### 3.10 σ_iptm does **not** track experimental potency — a negative anchor on n=93 ligands with quantitative IC50

Sections 3.6–3.9 establish that σ_iptm is a *real, reproducible, selection-robust* per-ligand property. They do not establish what it is worth. The decisive question — and the one a reviewer is entitled to ask of any reliability metric — is whether predictions the metric trusts agree with **experiment** better than the ones it distrusts. We tested this directly and report a **negative result**.

**Design.** We co-folded every ChEMBL MMP-1 ligand carrying a *quantitative* IC50 (relation '=', n=93; the 2 censored '>' records were excluded because a censored value is not a point estimate) under the identical protocol used for the 15-ligand panel — 100 diffusion samples per ligand, `--use_potentials`, seed 8107 — yielding 9,288 predictions. This is a separate and larger cohort from the reseed panel, run at the same protocol so the two are directly comparable. Experimental potency spans **IC50 = 0.2–840 nM (3.62 log units; pIC50 6.08–9.70, SD 0.81)**, drawn from 6 ChEMBL document sources.

**Result — no evidence that the metric predicts its own error.** Ranking ligands by iptm_mean and by pIC50 and correlating σ_iptm against the resulting per-ligand rank discrepancy gives **Spearman ρ = −0.005 (p = 0.960, 95 % CI [−0.21, +0.20])**. The corresponding pilot on the 15-ligand reseed panel (per-ligand mean σ_iptm over 161 cycles versus the same rank discrepancy, ligands intersected with the calibration IC50 set) gives ρ = +0.246 (p = 0.376) — nominally the expected direction, but not distinguishable from zero at n = 15. We state the strength of the present null precisely rather than rhetorically: a Fisher-z calculation on that pilot estimate prescribes **n ≈ 127 for 80 % power**, so at n = 93 this cohort carries only **≈ 66 % power** against an effect of that size. The 95 % interval around the observed ρ nonetheless **excludes the pilot point estimate** (+0.246 > +0.20). The defensible conclusion is therefore a bound, not an abolition: **there is no evidence of a gating relationship, and any true effect is bounded above at ρ ≈ 0.20** — i.e. this cohort rules out a moderate gating effect but cannot rule out a small one.

**Result — the premise is not satisfied.** The gating test presupposes that iptm carries potency information for σ to gate. It does not: **Spearman(iptm_mean, pIC50) = −0.075 (p = 0.473, 95 % CI [−0.28, +0.13])**, with iptm confined to 0.9228–0.9878 — every ligand is confidently folded. Splitting at the median σ_iptm (0.0044) accordingly separates nothing: the low-σ half (n = 47, ρ = −0.112, 95 % CI [−0.39, +0.18]) and the high-σ half (n = 46, ρ = −0.124, 95 % CI [−0.40, +0.17]) agree with experiment indistinguishably (**Fisher z = +0.06, p = 0.953**). Nor is σ a disguised potency surrogate (**Spearman(σ_iptm, pIC50) = +0.059, p = 0.571**), which would have made any apparent gating effect an artifact of potent compounds folding consistently. The half-split intervals are wide by construction (n ≈ 46 each); the load-bearing statement is the full-cohort bound above, not the half-split comparison.

**Alternative explanations tested.** *Restriction of range* — excluded: 3.62 log units of potency is ample dynamic range, so attenuation cannot explain the absence of signal. *Assay heterogeneity across source documents* — excluded as a sufficient explanation: the null reproduces **within a single document, i.e. a single assay** (CHEMBL1145787, n = 51, ρ = −0.135, 95 % CI [−0.40, +0.15]; CHEMBL1145069, n = 28, ρ = −0.076, 95 % CI [−0.44, +0.31]), though these within-document intervals are individually wide and are offered as consistency checks rather than as independently powered tests.

**Interpretation and scope.** This is not a defect discovered in Boltz-2; it is a boundary made explicit. The iptm head is a *structural interface-confidence* estimator, not an affinity predictor (§1.3), so the absence of a potency relationship is the expected behaviour of a correctly-used metric — but it has, to our knowledge, not previously been measured at this power on a metalloprotease, and it disciplines the claim this paper is entitled to make. **σ_iptm is a numerical-reproducibility axis for triaging which predictions are stable enough to act on; it does not rank potency among binders and must not be marketed as a potency predictor** — though §3.11, on a cohort that includes non-binders, shows it does coarsely separate binders from non-binders, a weaker and distinct function. The rescue protocol of §3.3/§4.2 is unaffected: rescuing CHEMBL94487 from σ_E = 14.27 to 0.007 kcal/mol is a statement about the reproducibility of a computed quantity, which this anchor does not touch.

Two limits bound the negative result honestly. First, the cohort's σ_iptm range (0.0012–0.0183, 15.1×) tops out **below** the reseed panel's outlier CHEMBL259829 (median 0.0548): these 93 ligands contain no genuinely unreliable member, so the gate had little to reject. Second — and more consequential — all 93 are **actives** (worst 840 nM); the cohort contains no decoys. This section therefore establishes only that σ_iptm does not rank potency *among binders*. Whether it discriminates **binders from non-binders** — the one function a virtual-screening gate must actually perform — is a separate question, which §3.11 answers on a cohort built for it. Source: `pilot/round27_paperA/anchor_sigma_ic50/anchor_result.csv` (93 rows: chembl_id, n_samples, iptm_mean, sigma_iptm, ic50_nm, pIC50); manifest `anchor_manifest.csv`; run script `scripts/round27_paperA/anchor_sigma_ic50_run.sh`; analysis script `scripts/round27_paperA/anchor_sigma_ic50_analyze.py`.

### 3.11 σ_iptm **does** separate binders from non-binders — a positive discrimination result across 5.1 log units

§3.10 answered a ranking question on a range-restricted cohort (all actives) and returned a null. The function a screening gate actually performs is coarser — separate a compound worth pursuing from one that is not — and it requires a cohort that contains both. We built one from a primary source and ran it at the identical protocol.

**Cohort.** `data/mmp1_panel_pubchem.csv`: 121 compounds retrieved from PubChem's SDQ bioactivity collection for gene MMP1 (geneid 4312), each carrying an IC50 with the assay AID it was measured in, the PubChem CID, and the retrieval timestamp, so the query can be re-run and diffed. Potency spans **IC50 = 0.78 nM – 98 µM (5.10 log units)**, and 36 compounds are weaker than 10 µM — an essentially-inactive arm that *came with the data* rather than from a decoy generator. This is a deliberate choice: these are real molecules with real measurements against the real target, so the negative arm carries none of the artificial-decoy bias a DUD-E-style set would, though it is correspondingly *harder* than decoys because its weak members are genuine weak binders, not non-binders. Co-folding used the same 100 diffusion samples/ligand, `--use_potentials`, seed 8107 (12,100 predictions), so this cohort, the n=93 anchor, and the 15-ligand reseed panel are directly comparable. We make no name or mechanism claim for any member; only the measured IC50 and the structure are used.

**Result — the confidence axis separates the arms.** Reading iptm_mean as a classifier of active-vs-inactive (ROC AUC via Mann-Whitney U, 2,000-sample bootstrap CI, null = 0.5) gives discrimination at **every threshold tested**, each CI excluding 0.5: AUC = **0.709 [0.611, 0.800]** at a 1 µM cut (52 vs 69), **0.684 [0.587, 0.777]** at 10 µM (85 vs 36), **0.744 [0.652, 0.830]** at the median 1.6 µM (59 vs 62). σ_iptm discriminates with equal strength in the physically expected direction — potent compounds fold *more* reproducibly — giving AUC 0.302 / 0.249 / 0.244 (equivalently **0.698 / 0.751 / 0.756** read as low-σ ⇒ active), CIs again excluding 0.5. Across the full continuous range both axes are informative: **Spearman(iptm_mean, pIC50) = +0.359 (p < 0.001, 95 % CI [+0.193, +0.505])** and, more strongly, **Spearman(σ_iptm, pIC50) = −0.443 (p < 0.001, 95 % CI [−0.576, −0.287])** — the *reproducibility* of the confidence, not its mean, is the better potency correlate. Median-split arm means: potent iptm 0.9753 ± 0.0070 / σ_iptm 0.0025 ± 0.0013 versus weak iptm 0.9695 ± 0.0073 / σ_iptm 0.0042 ± 0.0030.

**The signal is not an artifact of molecular size.** Heavy-atom count is uncorrelated with potency in this panel (Spearman(heavy, pIC50) = −0.054, p = 0.56), so the strong dependence of iptm on size (Spearman(heavy, iptm_mean) = −0.461) cannot smuggle a potency signal in through size. Partial rank correlations controlling for heavy-atom count leave both relationships essentially unchanged — σ_iptm↔pIC50 −0.440 (raw −0.443), iptm_mean↔pIC50 +0.377 (raw +0.359) — and a leave-one-out jackknife on the σ_iptm↔pIC50 correlation stays within [−0.467, −0.429] with no sign change, so no single compound drives it.

**Reconciliation with §3.10, and the honest ceiling.** These results do not contradict the anchor null; they delimit it. Among the 93 actives, iptm was flat against potency (ρ = −0.075); across 5.1 log units it is positive (+0.359). That sign change is textbook **restriction-of-range attenuation**: the screening-relevant variance lives between binders and non-binders, exactly the axis the all-actives anchor collapsed. The correct combined statement is therefore that **σ_iptm is a coarse binder/non-binder *filter*, not a fine potency *ranker***. And "coarse" is meant literally: AUC 0.68–0.74 is useful triage, not a strong classifier, and the entire effect lives inside an iptm band 0.95–0.99 wide — a metric to prioritise a queue, not to make a decision on a single compound. In the language §3.10 used, a positive result here *licenses σ_iptm as a virtual-screening filter*; the license is a modest one, and the confirmatory control below fixes both its floor and its ceiling. Source: `pilot/round27_paperA/discrimination_pubchem/discrimination_result.csv` (per-compound: cand_id, pubchem_cid, n_samples, iptm_mean, sigma_iptm, ic50_nm, pIC50, pubchem_aid); panel `data/mmp1_panel_pubchem.csv` built by `scripts/round27_paperA/build_mmp1_panel_pubchem.py`; inputs `build_discrimination_inputs.py`; run `discrimination_cofold_run.sh`; analysis `discrimination_analyze.py`.

**Confirmatory control — property-matched decoys.** The inactive arm above is *real weak binders* (IC50 > 10 µM), not confirmed non-binders, so it bounds but does not equal the discrimination a prospective screen faces. We therefore ran the cleaner DUD-E-style control: the 52 potent actives (IC50 < 1 µM) against 90 decoys drawn from ZINC250k (an MMP-1-neutral library), each matched to an active on six physicochemical properties (MW ± 25, cLogP ± 1.0, HBD ± 1, HBA ± 2, rotatable bonds ± 2, identical net charge) yet topologically distinct (ECFP4 Tanimoto < 0.35 to *every* active), co-folded at the identical protocol/MSA/seed. Here the separation is near-perfect: **AUC(iptm_mean) = 0.994 [0.985, 1.000]** and **AUC(σ_iptm) = 0.992 [0.979, 1.000]** (low σ ⇒ active), with maximal early enrichment (EF at 1 %/5 %/10 % all 2.73, the ceiling for this active fraction; BEDROC_20 = 0.999). The decoys are on average *lighter* than the actives (28.0 vs 29.5 heavy atoms), so molecular size works against the actives rather than for them, and the separation survives explicit size control — heavy-atom-residual AUC = 0.990 [0.978, 0.999], and in a logistic label ~ iptm + heavy the iptm term stays positive (coefficient + 15.3; the model sits in a quasi-complete-separation regime, so the residual-AUC is the decisive size-control statistic and the deflated Wald z is expected, not evidence against the effect). This is the discrimination result at its strongest — but it is the *easy* discrimination (off-chemotype molecules), and a near-perfect DUD-E AUC is a known ceiling, not a triumph: the co-fold model rejects non-MMP-1 scaffolds partly because they do not produce plausible poses in the pocket, which is exactly what a screening filter should do but is *not* evidence of fine affinity resolution. ### 3.12 The confidence axis dominates the energetic axis — pose-ensemble σ_E is a uniformly weaker filter

§3.10–§3.11 license σ_iptm as a coarse binder/non-binder filter. A natural question is whether this is specific to the interface confidence, or whether any ensemble-dispersion metric over the same 100 diffusion samples would do as well — harder-to-bind ligands might simply produce noisier ensembles across the board. We tested this by computing the **energetic analog of σ_iptm on the identical inputs**: for each compound we extracted the ligand from each of its 100 co-fold poses and took the standard deviation of its single-point energy across the ensemble (**pose-ensemble σ_E**, kcal/mol) — paper_A's reproducibility quantity, on the very poses σ_iptm is read from — with two independent potentials, GFN2-xTB (GBSA/water) and Orb-v3 OMol25, run across all three cohorts of §3.10–§3.11.

σ_E is a well-defined quantity: the two potentials agree on the per-compound values at **Pearson r = +0.982** (Spearman ρ = +0.813, n = 121), the real-compound counterpart of the MACE-OMol-0 ↔ Orb-v3 r = 0.9992 of paper_A §4.1. It reproduces the *shape* of σ_iptm's gradient — monotonically increasing discrimination as the negatives become more distinct — but sits **a full cohort-step below it at every level**:

| Cohort (increasing negative-distance) | σ_iptm | pose-ensemble σ_E (xtb / Orb-v3) |
|---|---|---|
| potency ranking among actives (Spearman ρ) | −0.005 (null) | +0.005 / +0.147 (null) |
| actives vs same-chemotype weak binders (52 vs 69), AUC | 0.68–0.74 | 0.52 / 0.44 — **no separation** (CI spans 0.5) |
| actives vs off-chemotype decoys (52 vs 90), AUC | 0.99 | 0.69 [0.60, 0.79] / 0.68 [0.59, 0.78] |

σ_E ranks no potency (xtb Spearman(σ_E, pIC50) = +0.005 [−0.176, +0.193]; partial-Spearman controlling heavy-atom count +0.012) and, unlike σ_iptm, **fails the screening-relevant same-chemotype separation entirely** (AUC 0.45–0.55 at every IC50 threshold, all CIs spanning 0.5). It becomes a filter only at the easiest cohort — against off-chemotype decoys — where both potentials agree at AUC ≈ 0.68, and the separation survives size control (heavy-atom-residual AUC 0.65 [0.55, 0.75]; the decoys are *lighter* than the actives, 28.0 vs 29.5 heavy atoms, so size works against the effect rather than for it). That ≈ 0.68 is roughly σ_iptm's *floor*, reached only where σ_iptm is already at its 0.99 ceiling on the same molecules.

**Interpretation.** Both dispersion axes are coarse confidence filters rather than potency rankers, but the interface-confidence dispersion (σ_iptm) carries substantially more binding-discrimination signal than the ligand-energy dispersion (σ_E): on identical inputs σ_iptm separates same-chemotype weak binders that σ_E cannot, and reaches near-perfect off-chemotype rejection where σ_E reaches only its own floor. The screening-relevant information lives mainly in the reproducibility of the *interface confidence*; the energetic axis contributes at most a weak off-chemotype filter. This also transfers paper_A's σ_E axis onto compounds with real, non-fabricated potency for the first time (§2.2): σ_E is reproducibly measured across independent potentials but is, at best, a weak binder/non-binder filter and no potency predictor — consistent with paper_A's "stability is not accuracy" position. ## 4. Discussion

The reproducibility benchmark of Wan et al. (arXiv:2603.05532) establishes that Boltz-2 is reliable in aggregate: across thousands of soluble-enzyme ligands, two independent inference runs correlate at Pearson r ≈ 0.91–0.96 on predicted affinity. Our findings complete this reliability picture by exposing the *per-ligand* structure that aggregate correlation statistics necessarily wash out — and by demonstrating that this structure resolves into **two orthogonal outlier-metric classes that each require a distinct upstream rescue intervention**. On a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease (MMP-1, 15 ChEMBL actives, **N = 161 reseed cycles complete v143–v303**, 100 diffusion samples / cycle / ligand = up to 241,500 cofold structures), we observe that the panel-wide intra-cycle σ-distributions are heavily right-skewed along *both* the energy axis (xtb GFN2 single-point) and the confidence axis (Boltz-2 iptm head): median ligand σ_E ≈ 0.01 kcal/mol and median ligand σ_iptm ≈ 0.003, but two ligands exhibit *orthogonal* outlier signatures. **CHEMBL94487** has σ_E = 14.27 kcal/mol at v15 — a 1,400-fold deviation that the iptm head fails to register (rank 5/15, intra-cycle σ_iptm max 0.0066, stable tier); pre-relaxing the v15 lowest-energy sample with **GFN2-xTB OPT** prior to co-folding collapses σ_E to 0.007 kcal/mol — a **2,068× reduction** — with v16 reproducing the rescued ensemble. **CHEMBL259829** symmetrically has max intra-cycle σ_iptm = 0.0643 (median 0.0548, 33.6× the most-converged ligand) — an interface-coordination ambiguity that the iptm head correctly registers but the energy metric does not. We therefore propose that Wan et al.'s "aggregate reliability" and our **"dual-axis individual rescuability"** are complementary tiers of a complete Boltz-2 reliability protocol: (i) report ensemble r as before, (ii) additionally screen per-ligand σ_E *and* σ_iptm at N ≥ 15 reseeds, (iii) for σ_E outliers, rescue via upstream GFN2-xTB OPT pre-relaxation; (iv) for σ_iptm outliers, triage via external structure validators (PLACER atomic-GNN, GatorAffinity SE(3)-GNN) before deciding to retain (alternative binding mode) or reject (physically inadmissible ensemble).

### 4.1 Why metalloproteins amplify the outlier signature

Three coupled features of the MMP-1 catalytic Zn²⁺ pocket conspire to amplify both the energy-axis (σ_E) and the confidence-axis (σ_iptm) outlier signatures characterized in §3.2–§3.3, and to make them quantitatively more severe than the corresponding signatures one would observe on a soluble (non-metallo) enzyme target of comparable affinity.

First, the **catalytic Zn²⁺ ion is a closed-shell d¹⁰ cation with limited electronic-anisotropy preference**. Unlike open-shell transition metal ions (Fe²⁺, Cu²⁺, Mn²⁺) that impose strong angular constraints on their coordination environment, Zn²⁺ is geometrically permissive across tetrahedral, distorted-tetrahedral, trigonal-bipyramidal, and square-pyramidal arrangements. The MMP-1 catalytic Zn²⁺ is anchored to His218, His222, and His228 via the canonical HExxHxxGxxH zinc-binding motif, leaving a single open coordination site whose geometry — and therefore the inhibitor coordination mode — is poorly constrained by the protein scaffold alone. This admits multiple plausible chelation geometries for any given inhibitor warhead, each consistent with the underlying protein structure.

Second, the **sulfonamide pharmacophore (Ar–SO₂NH₂) is a chemically ambivalent zinc-binding group**. The sulfonamide nitrogen lone pair can engage Zn²⁺ as an N-donor; alternatively, after deprotonation at the catalytic pH the SO₂N⁻H tautomer can chelate via either the deprotonated nitrogen or one of the two sulfonyl oxygens. Hydroxamate warheads exhibit the symmetric ambiguity in bidentate ({O, O}) versus monodentate ({O}) chelation. These competing modes lie within ≤5 kcal/mol of one another in many computational benchmarks, and the diffusion sampler legitimately explores multiple modes within a single 100-sample reseed cycle — surfacing as the σ_iptm signature observed on CHEMBL259829 (§3.3.2) and as the high-energy tail surfaced on CHEMBL94487 (§3.3.1).

Third, the **Boltz-2 training distribution is heavily weighted toward soluble, non-metalloprotein systems**. The PDB-derived training set used by Boltz-2 contains far more apo and soluble holo complexes than transition-metal-coordinated complexes of comparable resolution. The diffusion prior is therefore *under-constrained* at the catalytic Zn²⁺ vicinity relative to the rest of the binding pocket, and reseed-to-reseed σ is correspondingly amplified at exactly the location where physical chelation geometry matters most for binding-mode prediction. This argues that per-ligand σ-outlier auditing is **especially** load-bearing for any metalloprotein cofold campaign — not a niche refinement but a core methodological requirement that the Wan et al. (arXiv:2603.05532) soluble-enzyme baseline does not stress-test.

### 4.2 Why upstream QM relaxation, not post-hoc filtering

The xtb-OPT rescue intervention recommended in §3.3.1 and §5 operates *upstream* of the Boltz-2 diffusion model — relaxing the ligand input geometry *before* the next reseed cycle — rather than *downstream* by filtering or discarding outlier samples from a completed reseed. We argue that the upstream approach is strictly superior on three grounds.

First, **post-hoc filtering does not eliminate the underlying input ambiguity**: if the v15 ensemble of CHEMBL94487 contains a ~5%-occupancy high-energy distorted-coordination tail, discarding those samples after the fact leaves the *next* reseed cycle (v15-reseed-with-different-seed) free to re-sample exactly the same distorted population, because the same ambiguous input geometry feeds the same diffusion prior. The σ_E signature persists across independent reseeds with the same input. Empirically, paper_A v15 sprint replicates 1–9 of CHEMBL94487 (independently reseeded) all reproduced σ_E ≈ 14 kcal/mol; only the v16 cycle — which received the xtb-OPT-relaxed *input* — collapsed σ_E to 0.007. This identifies input geometry as the causal lever and filtering as a symptomatic treatment.

Second, **upstream QM relaxation is computationally cheap relative to the cofold cycle it rescues**. A single GFN2-xTB tight optimization on a drug-sized ligand (≤ 50 heavy atoms) completes in ≤ 30 seconds on one CPU core; a complete 15-ligand × 100-diffusion-sample Boltz-2 cofold cycle on the same hardware takes ~ 82 minutes of dedicated GPU wall time (§3.5). The xtb-OPT step adds < 0.7 % overhead to the cycle wall and < 0.001 % to total compute, while the avoided wasted Boltz cycle on a σ-outlier ligand recovers ~ 5.5 minutes per affected ligand of GPU time. Pre-relaxation is therefore *both* the higher-quality intervention and the cheaper one.

Third, **filtering loses molecules that may be biologically interesting**. The CHEMBL94487 σ-outlier is not a model failure on a dispensable compound — it is the model exposing a *legitimately ambiguous* coordination geometry on a clinically relevant ligand. Discarding the outlier sample throws away the signal; rescuing the input *preserves the molecule in the campaign* and allows it to be carried into downstream MD / FEP / wet-lab triage. For repositioning use cases — where the goal is to enrich for actionable hits rather than to maximize benchmark accuracy — the retention-via-rescue policy is the policy aligned with end-application value.

The conclusion of these three arguments is that **upstream QM relaxation is not merely *an option* among many post-hoc cleanup strategies — it is the only intervention that simultaneously (a) addresses the causal source, (b) costs less than the saved compute, and (c) preserves campaign coverage**. The two other reasonable interventions — extended sampling at N → ∞ to "average out" the tail, or ensemble-MD refinement of the ensemble — are both substantially more expensive and address only one of the three criteria.

### 4.3 Comparison with concurrent reliability literature

The reliability of next-generation cofold engines is a rapidly evolving topic, and the present work occupies a specific niche within that landscape — *per-ligand σ-outlier characterization on Zn²⁺ metalloprotease substrates with upstream-QM rescue* — distinguishable from at least four concurrent reliability strands.

**Wan et al. (arXiv:2603.05532, UCL Centre for Computational Science, Mar 2026)** is the direct prior art: a 38,482-compound dual-target (3CLPro + TNKS2) Boltz-2 evaluation reporting aggregate Pearson r = 0.913 / 0.962 across two reseeds on 793 / 799 ligand subsets, against ESMACS ensemble-MD ΔG ground-truth. That work establishes that *aggregate* affinity predictions are statistically stable across N = 2 inferences on soluble enzymes. Our work is *orthogonal*: we deepen the reseed axis (N = 15 → 28 with planned 25 reporting), shift to a *Zn²⁺ metalloprotease* substrate, characterize *per-ligand* σ distributions (rather than aggregate r alone), introduce the iptm-confidence axis (σ_iptm — invisible to affinity-only audits), and demonstrate a *causal* upstream-QM rescue (σ_E 14.27 → 0.007 kcal/mol) rather than post-hoc rejection.

**KinConfBench (bioRxiv 2026.04.07, 2,225 kinase chains × Boltz-2 / Chai-1 / Protenix)** reports that geometric pose success does not imply correct conformational-state recovery on kinase active-site loops — a *kinase-specific* observation extended here to the orthogonal *Zn²⁺ d¹⁰ coordination-permissive* failure mode.

**DeepUMQA-Global (bioRxiv 2026.04.15.718808v2)** reports +57.8 % Pearson / +49.0 % Spearman improvement over AF3 self-confidence as a third-party fold-accuracy estimator. We position DeepUMQA-Global as a *future* downstream validator complementary to the present per-ligand σ_E / σ_iptm framework; current paper_B uses GatorAffinity (SE(3)-GNN, 1.45 M complex pretrain) and PLACER (atomic-GNN ensemble, CSD + PDB training) as external validators instead, because DeepUMQA-Global at present (i) is trained on protein backbones only (ligand HETATM atoms untested for confidence outputs), (ii) the published weights ship as a PyTorch 1.11 build incompatible with RTX 5090 sm_120 kernel paths (cf. memory rule `rtx5090_sm120_torch_kernel_compat`), and (iii) software licensing for redistribution remains unresolved.

**Boltz-2x physicality-steering (Boltz Team 2025-12, paper_A v3 quantified 2026-05-09)** offers a complementary *training-side* intervention — flag-flippable physicality steering at sampling time, mean Δiptm = -0.22 % over 15 ligands × 100 samples — and on CHEMBL259829 specifically *worsens* σ_iptm by 2.4 %, suggesting that steering and upstream QM rescue address different failure modes. Both are compatible with the present framework but our protocol does not depend on Boltz-2x being enabled.

Across these four strands, the present study contributes the first **paired energy-axis vs confidence-axis σ-outlier characterization on a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease**, with axis-targeted rescue protocols. The aggregate-r tier (Wan), conformational-mismatch tier (KinConfBench), third-party UMQA tier (DeepUMQA-Global), and training-side steering tier (Boltz-2x) are each load-bearing in their respective domains; paper_B introduces a fifth, ligand-resolution tier orthogonal to all four.

**A sixth concurrent strand has emerged in 2026-Q1 specifically on the post-training-cutoff cofold-unreliability question** — namely, the Co-folded GPCR + IFD-MD rescue framework (ChemRxiv 2026.26434/chemrxiv-2026-1rkqz, January 2026), which demonstrates that GPCR cofold targets released *after* AlphaFold 3 / Boltz-2 training cutoff incur measurable retrospective FEP-rescue gain via physics-based induced-fit MD. This finding is *parallel-domain corroboration* of the paper_B dual-axis framework: GPCR (orthosteric small-molecule pocket) and Zn²⁺ metalloprotease (catalytic-metal coordination pocket) are mechanistically distinct, yet both surface a reliability gap that yields to upstream physics-based intervention (their IFD-MD ↔ our GFN2-xTB OPT rescue). Together with the Boltz-2x training-side strand and the present paper's ligand-resolution σ-tier, this establishes a three-axis intervention family (training-side / sampling-side / input-side) for post-train-cutoff cofold reliability — paper_B occupies the *input-side per-ligand* corner of that triangle, with the GPCR work occupying the *input-side per-protein* corner.

**Three additional reliability strands surfaced in 2026-Q2 (R31 frontier-tech scan 2026-05-22) sharpen the per-ligand-σ niche of the present work.**

**Protenix-v2 (ByteDance, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.04.10.717613v1, 2026-04-08)** reports antibody-antigen success-rate gains of +9–13 pp over Protenix-v1 and the striking observation that **a 5-seed Protenix-v2 ensemble matches or exceeds a 1000-seed Protenix-v1 ensemble** on the same antibody benchmark. This establishes a *seed-resolution efficiency* tier: weight-side training improvements can collapse the reseed-depth requirement by two orders of magnitude. For paper_B this is doubly relevant — it provides a concrete *deep-reseed-equivalence* benchmark against which our N = 28-reseed σ_iptm distribution can be reframed (deeper inference seeding partially substitutable by upgraded training), and it identifies Protenix-v2 as the natural successor cofold engine to which the present dual-axis protocol should be ported in paper_B v2.

**CalPro (Shihab et al., arXiv:2601.07201, 2026-01-12)** introduces a prior-aware evidential-conformal uncertainty-quantification head trained for finite-sample coverage guarantees on protein structure prediction. CalPro reports a **5 % coverage degradation across modalities** (versus 15–25 % degradation with vanilla MC-dropout or temperature-scaled baselines), **30–50 % calibration-error reduction**, and a **25 % downstream ligand-docking success-rate boost** when calibration is properly maintained across distribution shift. Where paper_B characterizes *inter-cycle* reliability (σ_iptm across N reseeds on a fixed ligand), CalPro characterizes *cross-modality coverage* (calibration retention as input modality shifts). The two are orthogonal: paper_B's σ_iptm framework remains the per-ligand outlier-detection axis, and CalPro becomes the recommended cross-modality calibration wrapper to apply downstream of the dual-axis triage. The present manuscript additionally adopts CalPro-aligned language in the Abstract σ-quantification statements for forward-compatibility.

**eMOSAIC (Zhang et al., *Nat Mach Intell* 2025, DOI s42256-025-01151-2, bioRxiv 2024.01.05.574359 v2)** introduces a Mahalanobis-outlier-scoring framework with a structure-informed protein language-model backbone for *multi-modal out-of-distribution individual-compound uncertainty* — explicitly per-compound, not aggregate. CHEMBL259829's σ_iptm = 0.0643 / 33.6× spread is precisely the signature class that eMOSAIC's individual-compound Mahalanobis scoring is designed to detect. The present paper_B framework constitutes the first reported *per-ligand σ-outlier identification on a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease*; eMOSAIC's Mahalanobis individual-UQ is the natural downstream OOD-classifier for the σ_iptm outlier class identified here, and a tighter integration (paper_B σ_iptm → eMOSAIC Mahalanobis OOD-score lookup) is reserved for paper_B v2.

Across all nine concurrent strands surveyed (Wan / KinConfBench / DeepUMQA-Global / Boltz-2x / GPCR-IFD-MD / Protenix-v2 / CalPro / eMOSAIC plus the present per-ligand-σ tier), the present manuscript remains the **only reported framework that (i) audits per-ligand σ in both σ_E and σ_iptm axes simultaneously, (ii) on a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease, (iii) with an upstream-QM rescue protocol that achieves a measured 2,068 × σ_E collapse**. The other eight strands are complementary at adjacent tiers (training-side, sampling-side, calibration-side, OOD-classifier-side, conformational-state-side) and are integrated by reference rather than substituted.

**A tenth concurrent strand emerged in early 2026 specifically on the latent-space steering axis** — namely, **Boltz-sample β-scaling** (Suzuki & Amagasa, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.01.23.701250, 2026-01-23 posting with five months of active development; GitHub `suzuki-2001/boltz-sample`). The Boltz-sample framework introduces a single scalar β rescaling applied to the latent pair representation at sampling time as a deterministic alternative to MSA-subsampling and heuristic perturbation schemes for systematically modulating conformational diversity. The β > 0 regime tightens the diffusion prior (analogous to increased constraint enforcement) while β < 0 broadens it (analogous to alternative-state exploration). This is **mechanistically orthogonal to the present per-ligand-σ tier**: where the present paper_B framework characterizes per-ligand σ_iptm under fixed sampling settings across N = 161 reseeds, Boltz-sample β-scaling instead varies the latent-space sampling parameter at fixed seed. For the CHEMBL259829 outlier (max intra-cycle σ_iptm = 0.0643, 33.6× spread), a paired β > 0 / β < 0 ablation against the v143–v303 dataset would distinguish whether the outlier reflects a *training-coverage gap* (in which case β > 0 should collapse σ_iptm by clamping latent diversity) versus an *intrinsic ligand flexibility* signal (in which case β < 0 should populate genuine alternative coordination modes). We propose integrating Boltz-sample β-scaling as a **10th reliability strand** — *latent-space steering tier* — and recommend its inclusion as the primary follow-on diagnostic for any σ_iptm outlier surfaced by the present per-ligand audit. The implementation is open-source, pip-installable, weight-compatible with the existing Boltz-2 environment used in this study, and dry-runnable within 1–2 GPU-hours.

**An eleventh concurrent strand surfaced in 2026-Q2 specifically on the standard-benchmark-scaffold axis** — namely, **FoldBench (BEAM Labs, *Nature Communications* 2026;17:442, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67127-3, paper_A ref 218)** — a comprehensive 1,522-biological-assembly × 9-task standard benchmark for AlphaFold3-style biomolecular structure prediction that evaluates AF3 + Boltz-1 + Chai-1 + HelixFold3 + Protenix-v1 5-model ensemble with explicit 558-complex protein-ligand sub-evaluation. The decisive FoldBench finding — "ligand docking accuracy notably diminishes as ligand similarity to training set decreases" — establishes the **standard-benchmark-scaffold reliability tier** that grounds the present manuscript's per-ligand σ-tier on a mainstream-journal community-anchored evaluation framework. FoldBench characterizes *aggregate cross-task* reliability under training-similarity stratification, whereas paper_B's per-ligand σ-tier characterizes *per-ligand inter-reseed* σ on a fixed metalloprotease target — the two tiers are mechanistically orthogonal: FoldBench measures the *average failure rate* across the training-similarity axis, while paper_B identifies *specific outlier ligands* (CHEMBL259829 σ_iptm = 0.0643 / 33.6× spread) within that average. The FoldBench finding *predicts* that CHEMBL259829's σ_iptm outlier should diminish as the Boltz-2 release used here is replaced with a successor trained on a 2026-Q3+ deposition window that closer matches the CHEMBL259829 chemotype — a falsifiable forward prediction that the FoldBench standard-benchmark scaffold makes available to subsequent paper_B v2 iterations.

With these two additions, the full reliability framework now spans eleven concurrent strands: aggregate-r (Wan), conformational-state (KinConfBench), third-party fold-quality (DeepUMQA-Global), training-side steering (Boltz-2x), input-side per-protein (GPCR + IFD-MD), seed-resolution efficiency (Protenix-v2), evidential-conformal calibration (CalPro), OOD per-compound individual UQ (eMOSAIC), latent-space steering (Boltz-sample β-scaling), standard-benchmark scaffold (FoldBench), and the present manuscript's per-ligand σ-tier across σ_E and σ_iptm axes simultaneously on a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease.

**A twelfth concurrent strand was added by R51-K split-conformal CWC benchmark tier** (TESSERA + CP-under-covariate-shift, paper_A refs 230-i + 230-ii) — distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantee complementing the present per-ligand σ-tier at the conformal-prediction-set membership axis.

**A thirteenth concurrent strand emerged in late 2025-2026-Q1 specifically on the memorization-corrected reliability axis** — namely, **Wu Z et al. Nature Communications 2025-10 generalization-not-memorization evaluation of protein-ligand cofolding** (DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-63947-5, paper_A ref 236) — a 2,600 post-cutoff PLINDER subset audit demonstrating that **Boltz-2 / AF3 / Chai-1 / Protenix / RFAA all exhibit training-similarity-dependent accuracy degradation**, with **Boltz-2 0.327 % cases producing severe structural anomaly failing automated processing** under chemically plausible binding-site Gly/Phe mutagenesis or ligand-similarity decrement. This evidence establishes the **memorization-corrected reliability tier**: where the present paper_B framework characterizes per-ligand σ_iptm under fixed training distribution across N = 161 reseeds, the Wu memorization-axis evidence quantifies the **train-set Tanimoto-similarity-weighted accuracy decay** as an orthogonal vulnerability dimension. For the CHEMBL259829 σ_iptm = 0.0643 / 33.6× spread outlier, a paired training-similarity-corrected σ_iptm decomposition — gating the outlier signal against Tanimoto-distance-to-Boltz-2-training as a memorization-residual vs genuine-novelty attribution — is the rationally-required v2 follow-on. The implementation is straightforward (ChEMBL-vs-Boltz-2-training Tanimoto distance + memorization-residual subtraction) and weight-compatible with the existing per-ligand σ_iptm pipeline.

With this thirteenth addition, the full reliability framework spans thirteen concurrent strands and the present paper_B occupies the per-ligand-σ corner on a Zn²⁺ metalloprotease that remains unaddressed by any of the other twelve strands. The present paper_B occupies the per-ligand-σ corner that remains unaddressed by any of the other nine strands.

### 4.4 Limitations

Seven limitations bound the present study's generalizability and should be addressed by follow-on work.

1. **Single metalloprotein target.** All cofold and rescue data are collected on MMP-1 (P03956, catalytic domain residues 100–269). The dual-axis σ-outlier framework is *expected* to generalize to closely related Zn²⁺ metalloproteases (MMP-2, MMP-9, MMP-12, MMP-13, ADAM family, ACE, carbonic anhydrases) and to other transition-metal active sites (Fe²⁺, Mn²⁺, Cu²⁺) where coordination ambiguity is similarly load-bearing — but this expectation is *not* directly validated here. A multi-MMP cross-validation panel is the natural follow-up.

2. **Panel chemotype coverage is not characterised.** The 15-ligand panel's members carry zinc-binding chemotypes (hydroxamate, sulfonamide-hydroxamate, carboxylate, thiol) by structural inspection, but the panel's provenance is unverified (§2.2) and the class-level framing previously asserted here rested on paper_A v6 sections that have since been withdrawn (see paper_A v0.3 §6.1). Whether the panel spans the MMP-1 chemotype space — and whether phosphonate, phosphinate or macrocyclic zinc-binding groups behave differently on the σ axes — is untested. Re-deriving the panel from a recorded ChEMBL retrieval is a prerequisite for any such claim.

3. **Reseed depth N = 25 is empirically chosen, not asymptotically saturated.** σ_iptm and σ_E distributions at N = 50 or N = 100 may surface additional outlier classes invisible at N = 25. The N = 28 cycle dataset reported in §3.2 ( a slight overshoot of the planned N = 25 target due to opportunistic continued sampling) does not show new σ_iptm > 0.07 outliers, but cannot exclude very-rare-mode outliers (one-in-50, one-in-100).

4. **No structural or absolute-ΔG ground truth — and, where experimental ground truth *is* available, σ_iptm does not track it.** This study evaluates Boltz-2 *reseed stability* (σ_E, σ_iptm, wall CV) and *rescue protocol efficacy* (σ_E collapse) — not the *accuracy* of any individual binding-mode prediction. Experimental **potency** ground truth is now collected (§3.10, n = 93 ChEMBL ligands with quantitative IC50) and returns a null: σ_iptm shows no relationship to potency-rank error (ρ = −0.005, 95 % CI [−0.21, +0.20]) and iptm is not itself potency-informative (ρ = −0.075, 95 % CI [−0.28, +0.13]) — bounding any gating effect at ρ ≈ 0.20 rather than excluding a small one (the cohort carries ≈ 66 % power against the 15-ligand pilot estimate ρ = +0.246). This bounds the metric rather than the protocol — σ_iptm is a reproducibility axis and at most a *coarse* binder/non-binder filter (§3.11: AUC 0.68–0.74 vs same-chemotype weak binders, ≈ 0.99 vs property-matched decoys), not a potency predictor among actives (§3.10) — but it means no claim of fine *predictive* accuracy is supported anywhere in this work. Absolute ΔG ground truth (ESMACS-style ensemble MD, as used by Wan et al., arXiv:2603.05532) and crystallographic binding-mode ground truth remain uncollected; the "rescued ensemble matches the experimental binding mode" claim therefore still rests on external structural validators (GatorAffinity, PLACER — §3.4) rather than first-principles or experimental validation.

5. **CHEMBL259829 σ_iptm rescue protocol is hypothesized but not yet validated.** The dual-axis framework prescribes PLACER + GatorAffinity triage for σ_iptm outliers (§5 step 4b). On CHEMBL94487 we report direct confirmed rescue (σ_E 14.27 → 0.007 collapse, 2,068× reduction). The analogous test on CHEMBL259829 σ_iptm — whether the high-σ ensemble represents an alternative-binding-mode worth retaining or a physically inadmissible ensemble worth rejecting — is the central deliverable of the paper_B v0.2 sprint scheduled for the post-D14 window (2026-05-30 to 2026-06-13).

6. **Hardware envelope-specific reproducibility measurements.** Wall-clock figures in §3.5 are collected on a single RTX 5090 GPU + 24-core x86 CPU under WSL2/Linux 6.6. Reproducibility of the n=15 baseline wall (88.4 min, CV 3.8 %) and of the n=10 acceleration plateau (81.2 min, –8.2 %) on other GPU classes (A100, H100, RTX A6000) or other operating-system envelopes is not characterized.

7. **Binder-vs-non-binder discrimination is modest, and the negative arm is weak binders, not true non-binders.** §3.11 answers the discrimination question the anchor could not: on 121 PubChem MMP-1 compounds spanning 5.1 log units, σ_iptm and iptm_mean do separate the active from the inactive arm (iptm AUC 0.68–0.74, all CIs excluding 0.5; the size confound is excluded by partial correlation and jackknife). This licenses σ_iptm as a virtual-screening *filter* rather than confining it to numerical triage — but the license is narrow. The effect is coarse (AUC 0.68–0.74, inside an iptm band only 0.95–0.99 wide): usable to prioritise a queue, not to decide on a single compound. And the inactive arm consists of real compounds measured at IC50 > 10 µM against MMP-1 — genuine weak binders, not property-matched decoys or confirmed non-binders — so the number that matters for a true prospective screen (separation from a chemically-matched decoy set that was never active) is bounded by this result rather than equal to it. **That confirmatory control is now run** (§3.11, *Confirmatory control*): 52 potent actives against 90 ZINC property-matched, topologically-distinct decoys, co-folded at the identical protocol, separate at AUC ≈ 0.99 (size-controlled; heavy-atom-residual AUC 0.990). The honest reading is that this is the *easy* end of the discrimination and the real-world figure lies between the ≈ 0.68 same-chemotype floor and this ≈ 0.99 off-chemotype ceiling — the filter license stands, a potency-ranking license does not.

## 5. Conclusion + Reliability protocol recommendation

We propose a **complete dual-axis Boltz-2 reliability protocol** for Zn²⁺-metalloprotease cofold campaigns:
1. **Aggregate Pearson r** on full library at N≥2 reseeds (Wan et al. tier).
2. **Per-ligand σ_E audit** (xtb GFN2 single-point energy across the 100 diffusion samples) at N≥15 reseeds on a stratified subset — surfaces *internal-geometry* outliers invisible to the iptm head.
3. **Per-ligand σ_iptm audit** (Boltz-2 iptm confidence head across the 100 diffusion samples) at N≥15 reseeds in parallel — surfaces *interface-coordination* outliers invisible to the energy metric.
4. **Axis-targeted rescue interventions**:
   - σ_E outliers → upstream **GFN2-xTB OPT** pre-relaxation of the v_N lowest-energy sample → v_{N+1} input.
   - σ_iptm outliers → external **PLACER + GatorAffinity** triage of the high-σ ensemble → retain (alternative binding mode) or reject (physically inadmissible).
5. **Wall-clock CV** as infrastructure reliability baseline (n=15 baseline mean 88.4 min, σ 3.4 min, CV 3.8% on shared paper_A/B infrastructure).

The CHEMBL94487 **2,068× σ_E collapse** demonstrates that energy-axis outliers are tractable via internal-geometry rescue. The CHEMBL259829 **33.6× σ_iptm signature** identifies the orthogonal failure mode requiring interface-coordination triage. Adopting this 5-step dual-axis protocol enables high-throughput cofold-based Zn²⁺-metalloprotease repositioning campaigns to **retain rather than discard** ligands that initially appear unreliable, and to **distinguish recoverable internal-geometry errors from irreducible interface-coordination ambiguity** — a distinction invisible to either metric in isolation.
